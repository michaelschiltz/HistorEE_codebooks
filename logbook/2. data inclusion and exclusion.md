## 2026-09-04 — ai — `avariz_vakfi` and `bruderschaft_salzburg` in; `confraternita` created as an umbrella and deliberately not coded

64 rows, `OF-0644`–`OF-0707`, both forms on all 32 characteristics, `coder=ai`, unreviewed. Three type rows. Proposed in `proposed-of/` by a session working from the blind bundle `_blind-mutual-pole-2026-09-04`, which did not write to the repository and did not run `git`.

**The scope decision the batch was commissioned to make, and it went against the commission.** The brief asked for `confraternita`, "the lay religious confraternity of Latin Christendom, treated as an organizational form". It is **not** coded as one row. `confraternita` is created as an uncoded umbrella and the coded child is `bruderschaft_salzburg` — the post-Tridentine Bruderschaft and Liebesbund of the city and archdiocese of Salzburg, 1600–1950, Klieber's *Fraternität*.

**The reasons are the sources' own, which is what makes the decision defensible rather than cautious.** Klieber contrasts his object with medieval predecessors on free access (printed 47–48), with the Venetian *scuole grandi* on political role (printed 61), and with the romance lands on the charitable dimension (printed 57–59). Gazzini restricts herself to Italian religious associations "only when they were mainly composed of professionals" (printed 173) and treats *corporazioni* and *confraternite* as contiguous and in some periods indistinguishable (printed 171). A row over both would take its values from two authors each of whom says the other's object is different, and any divergence inside it would be an artefact of tradition. This honours, before the fact, the standing instruction of 2026-08-13 — taken from this same Klieber article — that the loss-census `confraternity_fund` "cannot be one row for Latin Christendom".

**And only one of the two could have carried an `organizational_forms` row at all.** Gazzini answers almost nothing this dataset asks — no legal personality, no asset partitioning, no capital lock-in, no transferable claims, no succession — and she is already the source of `confraternity_fund_it`, so coding from her here would enter one witness twice. Klieber answers property, capital, governance, membership, entry, authorisation, succession and purpose. **The Italian confraternity is therefore absent from the entity census, and the umbrella row names the acquisition that would end that: Frank, *Bruderschaften als Bank* (`UNWASN6P`), which has no attachment and is the item that would have carried the capital questions.**

**A directed co-occurrence declaration was refused as false.** The brief directed `cooccurrence_basis=same-institution` against `confraternity_fund_it`. Under the scope decision that is not true: `confraternity_fund_it` is Gazzini's medieval Italian material and `bruderschaft_salzburg` is Klieber's early-modern Salzburg material. The relation that does hold between them is **evidential and not institutional** — both reach the census through one analyst's question set, the Hellwege volume's guild-and-confraternity mutual-support frame — and `cooccurrence_basis` has no value for it. Declared against the `confraternita` umbrella instead. This is the second time in six weeks that column has been asked for something it cannot say.

**The reverse declarations were written into `loss_mitigation_type.csv`, and one of them was deliberately withheld.** `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme`, `avariz_fund` and `confraternity_fund` now name their entity counterparts, at the `same-institution` basis each of those rows already carried. ## 2026-08-31 — ai — are `fraterna` and `compagnia` two forms or two halves of one term? Two rows are kept, on the *commenda*/*societas_maris* precedent, and with the same prohibition

No cell coded, no row proposed, no row merged or withdrawn. This entry decides a **scoping** question that was blocking the coding pass, and writes the scope notes the two type rows lack.

### The question, and why it had to be asked before coding and not after

`vocabularies/organizational_form_type.csv` carries `fraterna` (Venetian, 12-15c) and `compagnia` (Italian (Latin), 13-15c) as two rows with no coded cells. **The sources do not reliably use the two words the way the vocabulary does**, and if they name one form the census would code two rows and later withdraw one — the expensive order of operations.

### Disclosure, including one exposure incurred while writing this

The session's blind is spent under `fraterna-compagnia-acquisition` (logbook 4, standing table): it read the vault note *The fraterna and compagnia diverge in formation but converge at the barrier* in full, **and that note's title is a prediction of this entry's answer.** See the hazard section below, which is the reason this entry is written the way it is.

**A correction to logbook 1, 2026-08-31.** That entry states the session "has not read `data.csv` in either repo." That was true when written and is now **too strong**: validating the vocabulary patch required running `check_dependence.py`, which prints coded values, and its output disclosed the `LR4`/`LR5` pairings for eighteen forms and the form-name list (including `voc_1602`, `voc_1612`, `voc_1623`, `casa_san_giorgio`, `maona_chios`, `maona_corsica`, `partenrederij`, `bazacle_mill`). **No `AP1`, `AP2` or any `entity`-locator value was disclosed**, so logbook 1's substantive claim — that the locator rule was fixed without sight of the values it governs — stands. Logbook 1's caveat should carry this qualification.

*One incidental gain worth keeping: those form names are the sub-pool suspects the logbook 1 audit has to enumerate.*

### The case for ONE row

- **The Venetian term is *fraterna compagnia*.** The two codes name two halves of one phrase.
- **HKS 2006 printed 29 use *compagnia* generically**: "The medieval Italian partnership — termed the *compagnia*". Their evidence is Pisan, Sienese, Florentine and Genoese together, and their §V never uses "fraterna" at all.
- **Fleischer & Agstner 2017** trace partnership law to "the late medieval *commenda* and *compagnia*" — the same generic use, in a 2017 comparative-law survey.
- **Kohn 2003** lists "the *compagnia*" among the sea loan, *commenda*, share venture, joint stock, holding company and limited partnership. No *fraterna*.
- **Weber's sections are cities, not forms** — *Family Communities and Communities of Labor*, *Pisa*, *Florence*.
- **HKS printed 28-29 assert continuity, not contrast**: the *compagnia* "evolved gradually out of the laws and customs governing the household, as merchants' businesses initially grew by adding unrelated persons to the household." On that account the household form is the *compagnia*'s own earlier state, which is an argument against two rows and not merely against two labels.
- **The 53-paper deep search clusters by city**, Venice against Florence/Siena, not by two named institutional forms.

### The case for TWO rows

- **Formation differs categorically, and the matrix has columns for it.** The *fraterna* arises by **non-partition** of an inheritance — no act, no instrument; the *compagnia* by subscription to a term-limited contract. That is `MG1` (`membership` against `contract`) and `MG4` (`customary` against `private-contract`).
- **Duration differs**: no term against a fixed, renewable one to twelve years (HKS printed 29 n.85). That is `TS2` (`open` against `fixed-term`).
- **Capital differs, and is now documented on both sides.** The *compagnia* has a named, quantified **corpo** with a **sopraccorpo** above it — Hunt printed 113, partners' deposits "in excess of their capital contribution" at 7 per cent; Sapori printed XLIV, money deposited "*fuori del corpo di compagnia*". The *fraterna* has no firm capital distinct from the household estate. That is `CI1`.
- **Lane 1944** treats the Venetian family partnership as its own object across nineteen pages and **never once uses "compagnia"**.
- **González de Lara printed 109** distinguishes the *fraterna* as arising "without the need of formal contracts" — the contrast with a contractual form is the point of the sentence.
- **Ryabova 2018/2019** use "the Soranzo *fraterna*" as a form-name in current scholarship.

### The precedent that decides it, and it is not a new decision

**Logbook 4, 2026-08-30**, on `commenda` and `societas_maris`: the entity facet did not separate the unilateral from the bilateral *commenda* — the pair took identical values on all ten entity cells and separated only on `LR6` and `CF1`, both WP1. **The rows were kept separate**, against a literature (Pryor 1977, Luzzatto 1961, van Doosselaere 65 n.7) holding them "essentially the same agreement", **and a prohibition was recorded instead**: no comparative claim may treat the pair as two independent cases on any WP2 component.

`fraterna`/`compagnia` is the same shape one facet over. The forms are expected to separate on formation — `MG1`, `MG4`, `TS2`, `CI1` — and may well agree, or be `.NR`, across entity-shielding. **The precedent is to keep two rows and forbid the comparative claim, not to merge.**

> **DECISION: two rows are kept.** Neither may be treated as an independent case on any `entity-shielding` component claim until they are shown to take different values there. This prohibition is separate from, and additional to, the sub-pool prohibition recorded at logbook 1, 2026-08-31 — **and the `compagnia` is subject to both**, being a form with branches.

### The hazard, stated because this entry is exposed to it

The vault note is titled *The fraterna and compagnia diverge in formation but converge at the barrier*. **That is this entry's conclusion, written before any evidence was gathered.** The session that wrote this entry read that note in full.

The decision is nonetheless defensible, because it rests on a **precedent set on different forms by a different session** (*commenda*/*societas_maris*, 2026-08-30) rather than on the note's reasoning, and because it is the **conservative** move: keeping two rows preserves the option to merge on evidence, whereas merging now destroys the ability to discover a difference. But the coincidence must be visible to anyone auditing this, and the standing instruction follows from it:

> **The convergence half of the note's title is NOT established by this entry and must not be treated as established.** That the two rows may agree on entity-shielding is a *hypothesis the note supplies*, not a finding. It is exactly the claim a blind coding pass exists to test, and **a coder who finds the two rows agreeing on `AP1`/`AP2`/`LR1` should treat that agreement as needing more support, not less**, precisely because it was predicted.

### The scope notes the two type rows lack, and which the sources do not supply

Because the sources use the words loosely, the rows must fix their own referents. Proposed for `vocabularies/organizational_form_type.csv`:

- **`fraterna`** — the Venetian undivided fraternal patrimony: co-heir brothers declining to partition the paternal estate, which continues as a trading unit. Membership is co-heirship. **Not** any Italian family partnership generally.
- **`compagnia`** — the Tuscan/Florentine term-limited contractual partnership with a subscribed *corpo* and a *sopraccorpo* of deposits above it. **Not** the generic "medieval Italian partnership" of the comparative literature. **Where a source uses *compagnia* generically — HKS §V throughout, Fleischer & Agstner, Kohn — the coder must establish that the passage bears on the Tuscan contractual form before the cell takes a value from it, and record that judgement in the cell note.**

That last clause is the operative one. **HKS §V is the compagnia row's principal source and it is the most generic user of the word**, so the row's evidence base and its scope note are in tension by construction, on every cell.

### The period field, flagged and not changed

`fraterna` reads `12-15c`. González de Lara printed 109 has the *fraterna* "of little importance until the mid thirteenth century" (citing González de Lara 2008, 258-259). That is one clause in one source and does not by itself move a type row's period, but it is the only direct evidence the census holds on the question and the row should not be read as settled. **Press it against Lane 1944 before amending.**

**A finding from page 5, and it settles the shape of the `maona` boundary question.** The 1378 cession of Corsica is *formally* a feudal investiture — *in feudum rectum, nobile et gentile*, the six attributes of vassalic fidelity, a horse worth 40 gold florins each Pentecost *in signum subiectionis*, the doge touching the kneeling feudatories with the *baculus*. Petti Balbi's point is that beneath "questo rituale e questo formulario stantio" the clauses are those of *la società in partecipazione* and *il contratto d'appalto* of Genoese commercial practice — and **the language gives it away**: in one place *participes* appears in place of *feudatari*, in another *socii*. *Participes* is her stated technical term for those who, advancing money to the Republic, come to share in the collection of specified taxes.

### In: `commenda_alloc` and `qirad_alloc`, and they are the same row twice

`boundary_basis = contemporary-terminology`, `boundary_confidence = high`, on both.

**Every coded cell is identical.** `MC1=allocation`, `MB3=voluntary`, `RB1=capital-provider`, `RB2=capital-provider`, `RB3=none`, `RB4=1`, `PR1=0`, `PY0=.NA`. Nothing in the loss-allocation facet separates the *commenda* from the *qirāḍ*. That is not a shortcut; it is the answer to the question the brief asked, and it is what the sources say when read for this facet alone. Harris 2007 treats them as one institution under two names and his whole migration argument depends on it; Çizakça 2014 says flatly that "Islamic *mudaraba* was called *commenda* by the Europeans"; Ackerman-Lieberman 2011, 663 glosses "the *commenda* (*qirāḍ* or *muḍāraba* in Arabic)" in a single parenthesis. The vault note [[Isqa qirad and commenda are a liability-variable triad]] holds the trade environment fixed and varies agent liability; on **this** dataset's characteristics the liability variable does not move between these two.

The only cell where they differ is `VF1` — `documentary` against `.NR` — and that difference is a fact about the library, not about the forms. Both rows say so in terms. **Do not count these as two cases in any comparative claim.** The type rows carry the cross-reference and `cooccurs_with`.

Not coded thinly, though, and the reason is worth stating against the `ko_daimanin_1828` precedent (a near-copy coded on two cells so as not to inflate *n*). There the two associations were drafted by the same men in the same district; here the two rows are the same institution reached through two *literatures* that have been arguing about each other for sixty years, and the identity is the finding. Coding both in full is what makes it visible and falsifiable. The protection against inflating *n* is the declared `boundary_basis`, not a thin row.

### In: `isqa_alloc`, and it separates on substantive values

`boundary_basis = structural-difference`, `high`.

`RB2=shared` is the discriminating cell and the source sets it against the other two itself — Ackerman-Lieberman 2011, 663: "Maimonides holds the active partner responsible for **half of all trading losses**… The corresponding relationship described by Islamic law — the investment partnership known as the *commenda* (*qirāḍ* or *muḍāraba*) — holds the active partner indemnified from all partnership losses." `RB1=shared` follows at `medium`, because the manager is a *paid* bailee and so answerable for goods "lost or stolen" (662), but the source does not reach maritime peril or the *ones* exemption and the coding does not exceed it. `RB4=0` — the *pelga milveh* half is a debt that survives the venture.

So the triad is **2 + 1, not 3 and not 1**. The design the vault note describes survives, but it runs between the Jewish form and the other two, not among three.

### In: `societas_maris`, and the row was re-coded the same day when the Genoese source arrived

`boundary_basis = structural-difference`, `boundary_confidence = **high**`.

**First pass, before acquisition.** `RB1=RB2=shared` at `medium`/`analyst-imposed`, on one sentence of Harris 2007, 12 and on Held 2025's Ragusan material — a different city and, on Held's own conclusion, a different form. The entry as first written said this row was "the one to revisit first when van Doosselaere or Lopez & Raymond is acquired."

**MS supplied the van Doosselaere PDF within the hour, and it changes the row.** *Commercial Agreements and Social Dynamics in Medieval Genoa* (2009) is the Genoese notarial corpus: 6,764 *commenda* ties, 1154–1315, 93 per cent of all coded maritime ties. At p. 65 the two liability rules stand side by side as invariable terms of the standard contract:

> In a unilateral commenda, the investor collected three-fourths of the net proceeds and **bore all liability for loss**, while the traveler received only one-fourth of the net and bore no capital risk. In a bilateral commenda, **often called *societas* in Genoa** … In a bilateral venture, **the liability for loss was proportional to the respective initial investments of the participants**.

The traveller's contribution is itself customary — "always to the ratio one-third traveler, two-thirds investor". `RB1` goes to `high`/`articulated`, `RB2`, `RB3` and `RB4` to `high`, and `PR1=0` acquires its best evidence in the census (see logbook 4 (vi)). The row is no longer a placement.

**And it acquires a dissent that has to be recorded.** Pryor 1977, Luzzatto 1961, 119 and van Doosselaere 2009, 65 n.7 all hold that unilateral and bilateral *commenda* are "essentially the same agreement", the choice between them turning on the traveller's wealth. Every one of them reaches that on the **payout**, where the economics are identical by construction — van Doosselaere does the arithmetic at 65 n.6. **None of them reaches it on the loss, where they are not identical.** This census measures the loss, so the rows stay apart; the dissent is on the type row and in every cell note, and logbook 4 (v) works it out.

Still wanted for this row: Lopez & Raymond 174–184, which would supply the quoted clause van Doosselaere paraphrases.

### Out: the Ragusan collegantia as a regional split, and the queue entry is misfiled

## 2026-08-17 — ai (blind coding: Winkelman's Amsterdam bodemerijen)

Read 46 of the 48 notarial acts in Winkelman (ed.) 1983, *Bronnen tot de geschiedenis van den Oostzeehandel*, RGP Grote Serie 184/185/186 — nrs 946–962, 1991–2005, 3227–3242. Of the 46, **36 are `contract van bodemerij` and 10 are `protest wegens contract van bodemerij`**. No. 946 is wanting from the copy consulted and no. 947 survives only as a tail fragment; both are excluded. Winkelman's Inleiding on bodemerij was read with them.

### In: `bodemerij_amsterdam`, `LM-0512`–`LM-0524`

**One form, not several, and the reason is a missingness rule rather than a judgement of taste.**

The corpus varies on four axes that could each have carried a split. Pledge object: keel and gear, fractional parts, freight in its own right, goods and their returns, the borrower's whole estate, sureties. Pricing basis: a flat per-voyage percentage in 31 acts, a monthly rate in five, an absolute sum in one (no. 2002, 60 gld on 600, "maeckende tesamen 660 gld."). Duration: 30 single-voyage against six running by time with free navigation. Peril definition: a bare "op 't recht aventuyr van der zee" in most, an enumerated piracy clause in four, and in nos 3240 and 3242 a clause importing the whole custom of insurance — "alle anderen periculen, egeene uytgesondert, die een asseuradeur naer coustume van asseurantie is loopende", *haverije grosse* included.

That last group looked like the strongest candidate for a second form, because in it the lender bears **partial** loss pro rata ("penninck ponts gelijck") and takes pro rata of net salvage, where classic bottomry is all-or-nothing on the pledged thing. **It was rejected on the `.NR`/`0` rule.** The other 30 contracts do not deny proportional sharing; they are *silent* on partial loss. Splitting the corpus here would have coded that silence as a contrary value and manufactured a difference where the evidence shows only a difference in what was written down. `check_dependence.py` makes the same point in its own idiom when it marks separations achieved via `.NA` as weak.

Two further facts confirm the single form. The six insurance-referenced and piracy-clause acts cluster on **three parties** — Abraham de Velaer as lender, Marten Corsen as borrower, Dirck Woutersen and the pair Breugel & Smith — and on **eighteen months**, July 1618 to September 1621. That is a drafting habit of particular counterparties, not an institution. And the notary calls every one of them `contract van bodemerij`, in a register that also holds the plain ones.

### Naming and dating: Amsterdam, 1601–1621, and not Hanseatic

**The row is named for what was actually read.** The corpus is the practice of two Amsterdam notarial offices — Jan Franssen Bruyningh and Jacob Meerhout, with Epo Stoltenburch appearing once by inserted copy — over twenty years. Calling it Hanseatic *Bodmerei* or a northern European form would assert a geographic reach the evidence does not carry, and **Frankot is the reason that assertion is not available cheaply**. Her case is that Lübeck, Reval, Danzig, Kampen and Aberdeen used differing law in court despite holding overlapping written compilations, so a shared text never licenses an inference to shared practice. That argument is *methodological* here and nothing more: neither Frankot 2007 nor Frankot 2010 mentions bodemerij, bottomry or the sea loan at all, and it must not be cited as though it did. Jenks 2010 on Prussian Hanseatic trade finance points the same way from the other side — his instruments are *Wechsel* and *Darlehen* under a *Kreditverbot*, with no bodmerei in view.

The acts do reach beyond Amsterdam, and that is recorded rather than used. Money is taken up on bodemerij at La Rochelle (nos 955, 957, 959), Danzig (nos 960, 961, 1994, 1996) and Königsberg (nos 2004, 2005, 3238); borrowers come from Stralsund, Emden, Reval and Harlingen. **But every one of those instruments enters the record because an Amsterdam notary protested it.** The corpus is a window cut by Amsterdam procedure, and the sample is of Amsterdam enforcement, not of Baltic practice.

### `boundary_basis = documented-instance`, `boundary_confidence = high`

**What the value claims, and what it deliberately does not.** It claims that this row exists because a dated, localised body of transactional practice was read and coded from the acts themselves, in the way the `ko_*` rows and `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme` do. It does not claim a structural difference from the existing `bottomry` row, and that is not modesty — **it could not be assessed.** This coding was run blind and the `bottomry` codings were among the withheld material. The boundary against `bottomry` therefore stands open, and the maintainer should treat re-basing this row to `structural-difference` (or merging it into `bottomry`) as the first sighted decision after the blind is lifted.

The boundary against `sea_loan` **can** be stated from the sources and is not the same question. `sea_loan` is separated on `contemporary-terminology`; what separates `bodemerij_amsterdam` from it is a **constitutive pledge named in every single act** — "op kielscheeps ende 't gereetschap van dien", or on named goods — the pledge that the word *bodem* denotes. No. 3236 goes further and invokes a customary law for it by name: the money runs "op 't recht avontuyr van der see, op kielscheeps ende 't gereetschap **naer recht ende costume van bodemerije**". A contemporary Dutch legal form with its own custom and its own instrument, the *bodemerijebrief*, is not the undifferentiated *foenus nauticum*.

### Out: nothing, but three limits on the sample belong on the record

**The two missing acts are not random.** Nos 946 and 947 are the first two of the earliest run; the loss truncates the corpus at its early end, so any statement about change between 1601 and 1621 begins one contract late.

**The 150 contracts Winkelman counts are not the population of Amsterdam bodemerij.** He says so himself: much bodemerij was raised without a notary, and abroad the master could raise it under the *Plakkaat* of 1563 art. 19 without any Amsterdam instrument at all. He also gives the reason the notarial series is thin — the *partenrederij*, the free advance on freight, and marine insurance at roughly half the opgeld were all competing with it. **The survival is of the expensive, contested and formal cases.**

**Winkelman is a publisher's transcription, not the manuscript.** Every reading here is at one remove, the OCR of the printed page adds a second, and the edition's own apparatus carries at least one gap in the substance — at no. 3240 the percentage is simply absent, and the editor notes it: "Het percentage niet vermeld; blijkbaar overgeslagen." That cell is therefore not coded from that act.

## 2026-08-08 (iii) — ai (warichi)

`warichi_iwade` coded on 18 characteristics, `LM-0178`–`LM-0195`, from Brown 2006 and Brown 2011. Iwade village, Echigo — periodic reallocation of cultivation rights by drawn lot.

**`MC1`, `HZ1` and `HZ2` are held at `.NR` on purpose, and this is the substantive decision.** The reading of *warichi* as spreading flood impact is the **received view in the Japanese literature** (Furushima 1939; Aono 1982), resting on individual village studies. Brown 2006 tests it at regional scale with GIS over 113 villages and concludes that flood and landslide frequency "must be seen as insufficient causes by themselves, even if scholars ultimately see them as necessary conditions," and that explanation must be sought "more widely… than the threat of natural calamity stressed heretofore." Coding `MC1=pooling` would enter a contested orthodoxy as data; coding a `HZ1` value would assert a correlation its only regional test failed to find. Both are held open rather than resolved.

**`CN1` is `.NA` and that is the most informative cell in the batch.** *Warichi* has no contribution stream and accumulates no fund; it reallocates an existing asset rather than assembling one. Every other form in this census collects something — the *avería* levy, general average's ex-post assessment, the 掛金 of the *tanomoshi* associations, the tontine subscription. This is where *warichi* is unlike all of them, and it is the structural reason the pooling category fits badly. `CN2`, `CN3` and `LS2` propagate `.NA` from it.

**Best-attested cell is `PY2=rotation-lot`**: the village kept 「鬮替帳」 (*kujikae chō*, Lot Exchange Notebooks), and in 1756–57 the sixteen shares were organised into nine lot-drawing groups (*kujigumi*).

**Two characteristics fit awkwardly and are flagged rather than forced.** `PY0`/`PY1` record a draw by lot irrespective of loss, which is right about the trigger and wrong about the structure: all members draw simultaneously at each redistribution, and there is no queue or turn as in every rotating form previously coded. And *hikikuji* — the right to withhold land from the redistribution, running from under 2 per cent of village land in 1756–57 to 14 per cent in 1818, exceeding the 5 per cent Kaga allowed, and "concentrated in a few hands, not universally shared" — has **no cell anywhere in the vocabulary**. An opt-out from the allocation mechanism is not representable. Recorded in the `PY2` note and in logbook 6.

**Analytical framing kept out of the data.** The vault argues that *warichi* spreads the *incidence* of an irreversible loss rather than reversing it, pairing it functionally with general average, and that Brown's hazard variable measures exposure where a persistence measure is wanted. None of that is coded. See vault notes on incidence-spreading and on the equality-versus-dispersion test.

## 2026-08-08 — ai (organizational_forms)

`nakai_fictive_household` coded across all 32 characteristics, `OF-0156`–`OF-0187`. **The first Japanese form in this dataset**, and the first coded from an English-language source in it. From D'Amico 2026 (*Enterprise & Society*, `10.1017/eso.2026.10131`), drawing on the Nakai Genzaemon archive.

**Why the form is worth the coding.** Tokugawa merchants exploited the fact that an *ie*'s name need not correspond to a real person, and built fictive households that held assets, contracted, and were registered as households while having no body behind them. Nakai Iwanosuke "founded" the Osaka store in 1806 aged five, died in 1807, and went on paying interest on the main house's prospective funds until 1845 — over 20,000 *ryō*. Clerks cited his youth on the 1827 *shūmon aratame-chō* and switched to sickness in 1829 and 1835.

**Two cells decide things.** `MG4=private-contract` — no charter, no grant. The authorities enforced private contracts but designated partnership accounts *nakama-goto* and non-adjudicable, so these entities arose **against a standing refusal**, not merely in the absence of enabling law. `CI3=0` — D'Amico states flatly that Tokugawa houses "failed to develop joint-stock equity finance or transferability in the absence of any real use for these features." Shares (*kuchi*) existed and were proportioned; they did not circulate.

**What is now visible in the matrix.** `MG4` has all four values occupied for the first time (state-charter, customary, religious, private-contract). `FP3 identity wrapper` gains its first `1` — every other coded form is `0`, so the fictive household is the census's only pure identity wrapper. And `AP1=1` beside `AP3=0` gives entity shielding without limited liability in a single form, which is the Hansmann–Kraakman asymmetry observed outside the European lineage.

**The near-neighbour is `waqf_khayri`, which was not expected.** On the WP2 five the two forms nearly coincide: `AP1=1/1`, `TS1=1/1`, `CI3=0/0`, `CI2=1/P`. They diverge on `LP1` (0 against P), `MG4` (religious against private-contract), and `FP3` (0 against 1). A Japanese commercial network and an Islamic pious endowment arriving at the same entity-continuity-without-transferable-claims profile, from unrelated ancestral conditions and opposite purposes, is a candidate independent-arrival pair and should be assessed as one rather than asserted.

**Coded conservatively where the source says it cannot support more.** `LP3` is `.NR` because D'Amico writes that partnership matters were non-adjudicable, "so few legal sources survive", and "how liability worked in the case of bankruptcy for fictive household entities remains a mystery". `LR1` is `low` on a single case (the 1839 Karasu dissolution of Ōta-ya Uhe). `AP4` is `P` at `low` with the waqf-shaped caveat live — an *ie* inherited generation after generation has no founder's estate in AP4's sense. `MG3` is `.NA`: `MG1=contract` leaves no membership whose entry could be restricted, which `check_dependence.py` caught after an initial `.NR`.

**Verification queue.** The Sōma 1783 clause — the manager's share "to be used as a bonus for hard work; however, losses will be divided by 19 shares" — carries both `LR6` and `CF2` and is terse enough to read either way. Check against Egashira before either cell is used.

## 2026-08-08 — ai (loss_mitigation_forms)

Five Japanese rotating associations coded, `LM-0104`–`LM-0163`. From twelve Japanese-language items on 頼母子講 / 無尽講 supplied by MS.

**The unit is the named association, not the form.** `ko_mochiyori_yutsu_1876`, `ko_hokoin_1832`, `ko_usui_bango_1822`, `ko_shijunin_1829`, `ko_daimanin_1828` — each backed by a surviving 規約書 or 仕法帳. The abstraction `ko_mujin` in the type vocabulary is left uncoded and superseded.

**Why not an ideal type.** The first attempt was to split by function — 親頼母子 / 社寺講 / 村方講 — on the model of the tontine split. It does not survive the sources. 碓井村伴吾講 (1822) allocates 初会入札、弐会目振鬮、終リ迄一会替リ, alternating bid and lot round by round to the twenty-fifth; 四拾人講 (1829) allocates 振鬮を以 alone across forty-one rounds; 大満院講 (1828) inherits 伴吾講's rules verbatim because 岡田伊左衛門 and 塩野久右衛門 drafted the 仕法 for both. Same district, one decade, overlapping drafters, different allocation rules. **The allocation rule is a drafting choice by named 銀預り, not a property of the tradition** — so no ideal type can carry it, and no sentence of the form "Japanese rotating associations allocate by lot/bid" is supportable.

**Why not a period split.** MS's instruction, and the sources bear it out from a second direction: 松永 1999 on 頸城郡 shows collateral entering the same village form between Kyōhō and Hōreki–Meiwa, driven by *widening geographic scope* and consequent 信用が不安 rather than by date. Period would have encoded a developmental sequence the sample cannot support — the tontine error.

**What the association-level unit bought.** `articulation` becomes load-bearing: most cells now rest on clauses quoted verbatim from a 仕法帳 rather than on a modern description. The emic vocabulary survives in `notes` — 実掛/空掛/からかけ, 花鬮 and 華鬮, 振鬮, 講元/講親 against 銀預り/世話方, 当選銀, 満会, 席掛.

**Not coded, deliberately.** 榊原 2014 (無尽講の経済的意味) is a three-person three-period model, not evidence about how any kō operated; coding structural characteristics from it would be coding a model. Its result belongs in the vault instead. `ko_daimanin_1828` is coded thinly on purpose — a near-copy of 伴吾講, so full coding would inflate *n* without adding a degree of freedom. 松永's 赤沢村茂右衛門頼母子 (文化4) and 三浦's 法隆寺五十貫頼母子 (室町) are read but not yet coded; together they would give a Muromachi-to-Bunka span on the collateral question. 加藤 1998 is unread (820k characters, 落札 ×54) and is the deepest source on bid mechanics.

## 2026-08-07 — ai

`tontine` split into `tontine_en_1693` and `tontine_fr_royal`; `widows_fund_scotland` and `life_annuity_priced` added. `LM-0079`–`LM-0103`.

**Why the split.** The generic `tontine` row was entered with an explicit doubt recorded against it — that the label "probably spans pre- and post-actuarial forms, so one row may not hold it." It does not. The French royal series from 1689 divided subscribers into fourteen age classes carrying differentiated rates; the English tontine under the Million Act of 1693 did not differentiate by nominee age at all. Same mechanism, same decade, opposite values of `PR1`. One row cannot carry that, and smearing it would have destroyed the only result the form produces.

**What the split delivers.** `PR1` now has variance *inside* `MC1=pooling` at a fixed date, which is the falsification test `LM-0078` declared and could not run. It comes out against the sequence reading: pricing is orthogonal to mechanism, not a stage of it. This follows `CHARACTER-CODING.md`'s rule exactly — **add forms, not features** — and repeats the *asiento* pattern, where one new form settled in an afternoon what no amount of reasoning could.

**Why `widows_fund_scotland`.** Needed as the comparator, or `tontine_fr_royal PR1=1` asserts nothing. The Scottish fund is contemporaneous, also `MC1=pooling`, also priced ex ante — and `PY0=individual-indemnity` against the tontine's `individual-draw`. Pricing and payout direction therefore vary independently across the two, which is the discriminant-validity observation that `CHARACTER-CODING.md` test 4 says cannot be settled analytically. `MB3=compulsory` is coded `low` and unverified; if it holds, this is a compulsory pool *not* explained by non-excludability, which sharpens the open question standing against `averia_pool MB3`.

**Why `life_annuity_priced`.** The form that actually carries the pricing story — de Witt 1671 against the flat fourteen years' purchase, Hudde's Amsterdam registers, Halley 1693. Coded `MC1=spreading`, so it sits outside the pooling census and makes visible that the quantification episode belongs to a different mechanism than the one the tontine instantiates.

**Left alone deliberately.** `LM-0077` and `LM-0078` on the generic `tontine` type are **not** deleted or rewritten — schema and data growth here are additive, and the superseded coding should stay auditable, as with `cooperative_pooling_forms`. They are superseded by `LM-0079`–`LM-0093` and should be retired by the maintainer at review, not by the coder who replaced them. The `tontine` row in `vocabularies/loss_mitigation_type.csv` is likewise retained as a superordinate label.

**Not added.** `viatical` (out of period, though structurally identical to the tontine on `PY0` and worth the census if the argument runs to the present). `tontine_gb_1789` — the last major British issue, and the case that would test whether the English series itself moved to age classes before abandonment. Both parked here rather than coded from general knowledge.

**Provenance warning.** Every row is `coder=ai`, most carry `[verify]`, and the two figures the argument leans on — £108,100 tontine subscription against £118,506 in single-life annuities at fourteen years' purchase, and the French fourteen-class structure — come from secondary literature surveyed 2026-08-07, not from Exchequer records.
