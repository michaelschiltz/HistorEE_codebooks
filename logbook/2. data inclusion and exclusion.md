## 2026-08-18 — ai (blind coding: the four allocation anchors)

37 cells across nine characteristics. All four had zero coded cells; `ortoq_alloc` was left alone
on its own `key_source` determination. Blind session: the `exemplar` column, the 08-16 and 08-17

### The one decision that shapes everything else: nine characteristics, not twenty-six

The dataset holds 26 characteristics and these rows take 8 or 10 apiece. That is deliberate, and
the rule was fixed before the sources were read: **a characteristic already asked of the same form
in `organizational_forms` is not asked again here.** `commenda`, `qirad` and `isqa` carry 11, 11 and
32 rows there. Coding `LS3` for them would have restated `AP3` — the vocabulary says in terms that
`LS3` *is* "the direct analogue of `AP3` in `organizational_forms`" — and `LS1`/`LS2` would have
restated `LR3`/`LR4`. Three rows would then have "agreed" with three rows on evidence counted once.
The pooling machinery (`MB1`, `MB2`, `CN1`–`CN3`, `HZ1`, `HZ2`, `PY1`–`PY3`, `GV1`, `DR1`, `DR2`)
is inapplicable to bilateral contracts; `PY0=.NA` carries that, as it does for `sea_loan`.

What is left is what this dataset owns and the other does not: **`RB1` and `RB2`**, which split the
peril from the market and are the whole reason these rows earn their place — `organizational_forms`
`CF2` asks only whether the agent bears loss, not *which* loss — plus `RB3`, `RB4`, `PR1`, `MC1`,
`MB3` and `VF1`/`VF2`.

`societas_maris` is the exception in one direction: it has **no** row in `organizational_forms`
(nor does `mudaraba`), so nothing there is being restated. It is nevertheless coded thinly, for the
opposite reason — see below.

### In: `commenda_alloc` and `qirad_alloc`, and they are the same row twice

`boundary_basis = contemporary-terminology`, `boundary_confidence = high`, on both.

**Every coded cell is identical.** `MC1=allocation`, `MB3=voluntary`, `RB1=capital-provider`,
`RB2=capital-provider`, `RB3=none`, `RB4=1`, `PR1=0`, `PY0=.NA`. Nothing in the loss-allocation
facet separates the *commenda* from the *qirāḍ*. That is not a shortcut; it is the answer to the
question the brief asked, and it is what the sources say when read for this facet alone. Harris
2007 treats them as one institution under two names and his whole migration argument depends on it;
Çizakça 2014 says flatly that "Islamic *mudaraba* was called *commenda* by the Europeans";
Ackerman-Lieberman 2011, 663 glosses "the *commenda* (*qirāḍ* or *muḍāraba* in Arabic)" in a single
parenthesis. The vault note [[Isqa qirad and commenda are a liability-variable triad]] holds the
trade environment fixed and varies agent liability; on **this** dataset's characteristics the
liability variable does not move between these two.

The only cell where they differ is `VF1` — `documentary` against `.NR` — and that difference is a
fact about the library, not about the forms. Both rows say so in terms. **Do not count these as two
cases in any comparative claim.** The type rows carry the cross-reference and `cooccurs_with`.

Not coded thinly, though, and the reason is worth stating against the `ko_daimanin_1828` precedent
(a near-copy coded on two cells so as not to inflate *n*). There the two associations were drafted
by the same men in the same district; here the two rows are the same institution reached through
two *literatures* that have been arguing about each other for sixty years, and the identity is the
finding. Coding both in full is what makes it visible and falsifiable. The protection against
inflating *n* is the declared `boundary_basis`, not a thin row.

### In: `isqa_alloc`, and it separates on substantive values

`boundary_basis = structural-difference`, `high`.

`RB2=shared` is the discriminating cell and the source sets it against the other two itself —
Ackerman-Lieberman 2011, 663: "Maimonides holds the active partner responsible for **half of all
trading losses**… The corresponding relationship described by Islamic law — the investment
partnership known as the *commenda* (*qirāḍ* or *muḍāraba*) — holds the active partner indemnified
from all partnership losses." `RB1=shared` follows at `medium`, because the manager is a *paid*
bailee and so answerable for goods "lost or stolen" (662), but the source does not reach maritime
peril or the *ones* exemption and the coding does not exceed it. `RB4=0` — the *pelga milveh* half
is a debt that survives the venture.

So the triad is **2 + 1, not 3 and not 1**. The design the vault note describes survives, but it
runs between the Jewish form and the other two, not among three.

### In: `societas_maris`, and the row was re-coded the same day when the Genoese source arrived

`boundary_basis = structural-difference`, `boundary_confidence = **high**`.

**First pass, before acquisition.** `RB1=RB2=shared` at `medium`/`analyst-imposed`, on one sentence
of Harris 2007, 12 and on Held 2025's Ragusan material — a different city and, on Held's own
conclusion, a different form. The entry as first written said this row was "the one to revisit first
when van Doosselaere or Lopez & Raymond is acquired."

**MS supplied the van Doosselaere PDF within the hour, and it changes the row.** *Commercial
Agreements and Social Dynamics in Medieval Genoa* (2009) is the Genoese notarial corpus: 6,764
*commenda* ties, 1154–1315, 93 per cent of all coded maritime ties. At p. 65 the two liability rules
stand side by side as invariable terms of the standard contract:

> In a unilateral commenda, the investor collected three-fourths of the net proceeds and **bore all
> liability for loss**, while the traveler received only one-fourth of the net and bore no capital
> risk. In a bilateral commenda, **often called *societas* in Genoa** … In a bilateral venture, **the
> liability for loss was proportional to the respective initial investments of the participants**.

The traveller's contribution is itself customary — "always to the ratio one-third traveler,
two-thirds investor". `RB1` goes to `high`/`articulated`, `RB2`, `RB3` and `RB4` to `high`, and
`PR1=0` acquires its best evidence in the census (see logbook 4 (vi)). The row is no longer a
placement.

**And it acquires a dissent that has to be recorded.** Pryor 1977, Luzzatto 1961, 119 and van
Doosselaere 2009, 65 n.7 all hold that unilateral and bilateral *commenda* are "essentially the same
agreement", the choice between them turning on the traveller's wealth. Every one of them reaches
that on the **payout**, where the economics are identical by construction — van Doosselaere does the
arithmetic at 65 n.6. **None of them reaches it on the loss, where they are not identical.** This
census measures the loss, so the rows stay apart; the dissent is on the type row and in every cell
note, and logbook 4 (v) works it out.

Still wanted for this row: Lopez & Raymond 174–184, which would supply the quoted clause van
Doosselaere paraphrases.

### Out: the Ragusan collegantia as a regional split, and the queue entry is misfiled

Logbook 2 (2026-08-16) queued "Ragusan collegantia" as a regional split from `societas_maris` on the
`tontine_en`/`tontine_fr` precedent. **Not done, and it could not be done as queued.** Held 2025's
conclusion is that "the contract of *collegantia* in the late medieval law of Dubrovnik is a local
variant of the **unilateral** *commenda*" (23). Meanwhile in Venetian documents the term is used
"exclusively for the contract of bilateral *commenda*", and the unilateral form has no designation
at all (9, after Pryor 1977, 10). So:

- the row's title is coherent — Genoese *societas maris* and **Venetian** *collegantia* do both name
  the bilateral form;
- the **Ragusan** *collegantia*, despite being modelled on the Venetian one, is unilateral, so if it
  were ever split it would split from `commenda_alloc`;
- and it should not be split now. It agrees with `commenda_alloc` on every cell I can code. The
  `tontine` precedent licensed a split because the two halves took **opposite** values of `PR1` at a
  fixed date; there is no such contrast here.

The Ragusan material is therefore used as *evidence for* `commenda_alloc`, not as a form.

### Out: a fifth form the Dubrovnik corpus does contain, and why it is not created

Held's "local" and "undefined" categories carry the clause *salvos in terra*, sometimes with *sine
aliquo periculo dicti [N.]* — the tractator must repay "even if it was lost due to the mentioned
extraordinary, unforeseen events" (21). Nineteen of the twenty-three undefined contracts and three
of the local ones. Margetić reads the clause as turning the contract "basically into loans", and
Held resists only on the ground that the rest of the structure is retained.

Not coded, for the reason the *warichi* entry gives: it would enter a contested reading as data. But
the observation is worth more than the row would have been and it goes in logbook 4 — **in this
corpus the contract's name and its risk allocation are the same fact**: `collegantia` and *salvos in
terra* "are mutually exclusive", both sit immediately after the sum, and Held concludes that
"designation of a contract as *collegantia* would thus necessarily imply a particular allocation of
risks" (23). `RB1` is a contemporary legal category here, not an analyst's.

### Out: nothing else, but three limits on the batch belong on the record

1. **The doctrinal/documentary split is coded as doctrine and flagged, not resolved.**
   Ackerman-Lieberman 2011, 664: "legal documents from the Geniza concerning investment partnerships

   the active partner against losses." On his reading those are *ʿisqa*-shaped; on Udovitch's and
   Goitein's they are *qirāḍ* in practice, in which case `qirad_alloc RB2=capital-provider` is

2. **`PR1=0` on all four rejects a live alternative reading** — that the profit ratio embeds a

3. **`MC1`'s definition strains on two of these rows** and both say so: `allocation` "assigns the

   allocation form that apportions rather than assigns**, at which point the question is whether
   `MC1` needs a companion recording whether the risk is assigned entire or divided.

## 2026-08-17 — ai (blind coding: Winkelman's Amsterdam bodemerijen)

Read 46 of the 48 notarial acts in Winkelman (ed.) 1983, *Bronnen tot de geschiedenis van
den Oostzeehandel*, RGP Grote Serie 184/185/186 — nrs 946–962, 1991–2005, 3227–3242. Of
the 46, **36 are `contract van bodemerij` and 10 are `protest wegens contract van
bodemerij`**. No. 946 is wanting from the copy consulted and no. 947 survives only as a
tail fragment; both are excluded. Winkelman's Inleiding on bodemerij was read with them.

### In: `bodemerij_amsterdam`, `LM-0512`–`LM-0524`

**One form, not several, and the reason is a missingness rule rather than a judgement of
taste.**

The corpus varies on four axes that could each have carried a split. Pledge object: keel
and gear, fractional parts, freight in its own right, goods and their returns, the
borrower's whole estate, sureties. Pricing basis: a flat per-voyage percentage in 31 acts,
a monthly rate in five, an absolute sum in one (no. 2002, 60 gld on 600, "maeckende
tesamen 660 gld."). Duration: 30 single-voyage against six running by time with free
navigation. Peril definition: a bare "op 't recht aventuyr van der zee" in most, an
enumerated piracy clause in four, and in nos 3240 and 3242 a clause importing the whole
custom of insurance — "alle anderen periculen, egeene uytgesondert, die een asseuradeur
naer coustume van asseurantie is loopende", *haverije grosse* included.

That last group looked like the strongest candidate for a second form, because in it the
lender bears **partial** loss pro rata ("penninck ponts gelijck") and takes pro rata of net
salvage, where classic bottomry is all-or-nothing on the pledged thing. **It was rejected
on the `.NR`/`0` rule.** The other 30 contracts do not deny proportional sharing; they are
*silent* on partial loss. Splitting the corpus here would have coded that silence as a
contrary value and manufactured a difference where the evidence shows only a difference in
what was written down. `check_dependence.py` makes the same point in its own idiom when it
marks separations achieved via `.NA` as weak.

Two further facts confirm the single form. The six insurance-referenced and piracy-clause
acts cluster on **three parties** — Abraham de Velaer as lender, Marten Corsen as borrower,
Dirck Woutersen and the pair Breugel & Smith — and on **eighteen months**, July 1618 to
September 1621. That is a drafting habit of particular counterparties, not an institution.
And the notary calls every one of them `contract van bodemerij`, in a register that also
holds the plain ones.

### Naming and dating: Amsterdam, 1601–1621, and not Hanseatic

**The row is named for what was actually read.** The corpus is the practice of two
Amsterdam notarial offices — Jan Franssen Bruyningh and Jacob Meerhout, with Epo
Stoltenburch appearing once by inserted copy — over twenty years. Calling it Hanseatic
*Bodmerei* or a northern European form would assert a geographic reach the evidence does
not carry, and **Frankot is the reason that assertion is not available cheaply**. Her case
is that Lübeck, Reval, Danzig, Kampen and Aberdeen used differing law in court despite
holding overlapping written compilations, so a shared text never licenses an inference to
shared practice. That argument is *methodological* here and nothing more: neither Frankot
2007 nor Frankot 2010 mentions bodemerij, bottomry or the sea loan at all, and it must not
be cited as though it did. Jenks 2010 on Prussian Hanseatic trade finance points the same
way from the other side — his instruments are *Wechsel* and *Darlehen* under a
*Kreditverbot*, with no bodmerei in view.

The acts do reach beyond Amsterdam, and that is recorded rather than used. Money is taken
up on bodemerij at La Rochelle (nos 955, 957, 959), Danzig (nos 960, 961, 1994, 1996) and
Königsberg (nos 2004, 2005, 3238); borrowers come from Stralsund, Emden, Reval and
Harlingen. **But every one of those instruments enters the record because an Amsterdam
notary protested it.** The corpus is a window cut by Amsterdam procedure, and the sample is
of Amsterdam enforcement, not of Baltic practice.

### `boundary_basis = documented-instance`, `boundary_confidence = high`

**What the value claims, and what it deliberately does not.** It claims that this row
exists because a dated, localised body of transactional practice was read and coded from
the acts themselves, in the way the `ko_*` rows and `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme` do. It does
not claim a structural difference from the existing `bottomry` row, and that is not
modesty — **it could not be assessed.** This coding was run blind and the `bottomry`
codings were among the withheld material. The boundary against `bottomry` therefore stands
open, and the maintainer should treat re-basing this row to `structural-difference` (or
merging it into `bottomry`) as the first sighted decision after the blind is lifted.

The boundary against `sea_loan` **can** be stated from the sources and is not the same
question. `sea_loan` is separated on `contemporary-terminology`; what separates
`bodemerij_amsterdam` from it is a **constitutive pledge named in every single act** —
"op kielscheeps ende 't gereetschap van dien", or on named goods — the pledge that the word
*bodem* denotes. No. 3236 goes further and invokes a customary law for it by name: the
money runs "op 't recht avontuyr van der see, op kielscheeps ende 't gereetschap **naer
recht ende costume van bodemerije**". A contemporary Dutch legal form with its own custom
and its own instrument, the *bodemerijebrief*, is not the undifferentiated *foenus
nauticum*.

### Out: nothing, but three limits on the sample belong on the record

**The two missing acts are not random.** Nos 946 and 947 are the first two of the earliest
run; the loss truncates the corpus at its early end, so any statement about change between
1601 and 1621 begins one contract late.

**The 150 contracts Winkelman counts are not the population of Amsterdam bodemerij.** He
says so himself: much bodemerij was raised without a notary, and abroad the master could
raise it under the *Plakkaat* of 1563 art. 19 without any Amsterdam instrument at all. He
also gives the reason the notarial series is thin — the *partenrederij*, the free advance
on freight, and marine insurance at roughly half the opgeld were all competing with it.
**The survival is of the expensive, contested and formal cases.**

**Winkelman is a publisher's transcription, not the manuscript.** Every reading here is at
one remove, the OCR of the printed page adds a second, and the edition's own apparatus
carries at least one gap in the substance — at no. 3240 the percentage is simply absent,
and the editor notes it: "Het percentage niet vermeld; blijkbaar overgeslagen." That cell
is therefore not coded from that act.

## 2026-08-16 — ai, decision by MS — the maritime rows

`bottomry` is retained as a distinct form and its `boundary_basis` is `contemporary-legal-form` at
`high` confidence: attested in the Statutes of Marseille 1253/55 (*quod dictum pignus eat in viagio
ad fortunam creditoris*), legislated in the Hanserezess of 1418, named in the Amsterdam Statute of
1527, with Lübeck *Bodmereibriefe* surviving from the 15th century. Its differentia is the
constitutive pledge and the non-recourse (*dingliche*) liability — which is **`LS3`**, not `RB3`.

`respondentia` is retained but recorded as `analyst-split` at `medium`: *bodeme* is a collective term
for ship **and** cargo, no continental tradition subdivides the institution, and Steckley's sample of
the English High Court of Admiralty 1608–1699 yields 58 bottomry cases against one footnote mention
of respondentia. Retained rather than merged, but **it must not be counted as an independent form in
any diversity-budget argument.**

Two forms proposed for coding, in priority order:

1. **Hanseatic `bodmerei`** — Lübeck *Bodmereibriefe* (Pauli III, nos. 247ff.), Visby art. 40,
   Hamburg Stadtrecht 1603 II.14. Buys `PR1=1` **articulated**, `LS3=0`, and a jurisdiction outside
   effective canon-law reach: the control case against the Mediterranean concealed premium.
2. **Ragusan `collegantia`** — a regional split from `societas_maris` on the `tontine_en`/
   `tontine_fr` precedent; Held 2025 from the Statute of Dubrovnik 1272 and the *Monumenta historica
   Ragusina*, separating from the Venetian on profit division.

## 2026-08-13 — ai (three PDFs read, one coded, two excluded)

### In: `friendly_society_female_england`, `LM-0466`–`LM-0488`

From Rusnock & Dietz 2012, working from thirty-nine sets of printed rules for English
female friendly societies, 1780–1830.

**Coded as a new form rather than used to fill `friendly_society_england`'s `.NR` cells,
and that was the decision worth making.** The `friendly_society_england` type note said in
terms that "Rusnock & Dietz 2012 and Broten 2012 are in the collection and would fill CN2
and PR1." That instruction was written before the sources were read and it was wrong.
Prom's lodges are male or mixed and his window is 1870–1914; these are women's clubs a
century earlier, in a different legal position — married women "who had no independent
standing in law" none the less held office and administered funds. Filling one form's
missingness from another form's source would have manufactured agreement between two
populations never observed together, and would have destroyed exactly the comparison the
second source makes possible. **The two forms now separate on three cells:** `CN3` (`P`
against `1` — Prom's lodges paid members weeks in arrears, these levied and collected),
`PY3` (`1` against `P`), and `PR1` (`0` against `P`). Filling would have produced identity
by construction on all three.

**Two cells are new to the census.** `PY3=P` is the first **tapered** benefit — five
shillings a week for four weeks then two "until she recovered" at St Mary Redcliff; three
shillings for thirteen weeks then "1s. a week only, *while it is thought necessary*" in
Hampshire. The characteristic's ternary asks whether the payout is capped; it cannot
express a cap that changes shape over the life of a claim, and the note carries what the
cell cannot. `PR1=P` is the clearest ex-ante peril pricing yet found in a membership fund:
entry screened on health and capped at forty, and where the age bar was relaxed it was
relaxed **for a price** — older women admitted "only in exchange for higher entrance fees."
No table, no distribution; a charge graded against expected claim, which is the reading
already given to `tontine_fr_royal`.

**`MC1=pooling` is `medium`, not `high`, and for a new reason.** These societies had a
class of contributors who took nothing back: York's rules divide the membership into
"Two Classes, viz. Honorary Members" and "General Members," the honorary paying six
shillings a year for a committee seat while "exempt from fines and received no benefits";
Stafford's 1797 roster shows 65 honorary against 111 subscribing. That is the **second**
mechanism by which contributors fall outside Harris's pooling as written, after the
*noodhulpen* and civic levies of `guild_box_dutch` — and unlike those it is internal,
constitutional and named in the rules. Two instances is not yet a case for touching `MC1`.
**Falsification condition: a third form with non-beneficiary contributors inside the
membership, at which point the question is whether `MC1` needs a companion recording
whether the contributor set and the beneficiary set coincide.**

### Out: Lavallée, Bénézet & Julien 1993 — **and my description of it was wrong**

Catalogued and briefed as "the Rouen pharmacy guild … a focused case on managing
illness-cost reimbursements." **It is nothing of the kind, and I repeated Undermind's
summary without checking it — in conversation, and in the coding brief written into the
Undermind workspace.** *La maîtrise Notre-Dame de Rouen* is the cathedral **choir school**;
the article appeared in the *Revue d'Histoire de la Pharmacie* because it itemises apothecary
bills. Its subject is the cathedral chapter reimbursing the medical costs of its
*enfants de chœur* — payments to parents for "la dépense pour la maladie" and "les
remèdes," to the *maître de musique* for sums advanced, to the widow Vauquelin for nursing
the sick boys, to a butcher for meat supplied to one of them.

**Excluded because there is no pool.** No membership, no contribution stream, no fund
assembled from those exposed. An employer meets the medical costs of its dependants from
general revenue. `MC1` would fail at the first question and `CN1` would be `.NA` for the
same reason `warichi_iwade`'s is — nothing is collected. Coding it would have entered
institutional provision into a census of cooperative pooling and inflated *n* with a case
that shares only the payout.

**What it would nevertheless be good for, if the project ever needs it:** it is a clean
instance of *third-party* provision against the same peril the friendly societies pooled
against, at the same social scale, which makes it a comparator for what pooling was an
alternative *to*. Parked, not coded.

**The methodological point, since it cost nothing to learn here and could cost a great
deal later: a search engine's one-line characterisation of a source is not a finding, and
must not be repeated into a codebook before the source is opened.** Two of the twenty-three
items in the guild-and-fraternity sweep have now turned out to be mis-described in ways
that survived into repository prose — Goddard & Smalley, billed as evidence on refusal of
relief and containing none, and this one.

### Out: Klieber 2001 — excluded on the source's own finding

*Versicherungen fürs Fegefeuer*, on Salzburg confraternities and *Liebesbünde*, 1600–1950.
Acquired to extend `confraternity_fund` past Gazzini's Italian evidence. **It does the
opposite, which is worth more than the coding would have been.** Klieber's post-Tridentine
Salzburg fraternities are death-service institutions — insurance for purgatory, as the
title has it — whose material relief is "ein eher bescheidenes und eindeutig nachrangiges
Engagement": a court lackeys' *Liebesbund* reserved up to ten per cent of expenditure for
alms, and only from the 1770s. He states the regional limit explicitly: the medieval
fraternal tradition pointed further in that direction but tied it to *totendienstliche
Agenden*, and "diese karitative Dimension der Fraternitäten hat sich in romanischen Ländern
offensichtlich stärker erhalten."

**Not coded, for two reasons.** First, a form whose relief is a tenth of outgoings in one
house in one decade would carry `MC1` only by straining it, and the strain would be
invisible once the row was in the matrix. Second, the PDF's text layer is column-interleaved
to the point of unreliability — the passages above were legible, the *Beitragssätze* and
*Armenklauseln* discussions were not — and coding cells from OCR I cannot read straight
would put unverifiable values into the census. **If it is wanted, it needs the image route.**

**What is recorded instead** is the finding itself, on the `confraternity_fund` type row:
Gazzini's Italian pattern does not generalise to the German lands, so any future coding of
`confraternity_fund` must be regional and not a single row for Latin Christendom.

## 2026-08-08 (iii) — ai (warichi)

`warichi_iwade` coded on 18 characteristics, `LM-0178`–`LM-0195`, from Brown 2006 and Brown 2011. Iwade village, Echigo — periodic reallocation of cultivation rights by drawn lot.

**`MC1`, `HZ1` and `HZ2` are held at `.NR` on purpose, and this is the substantive decision.** The reading of *warichi* as spreading flood impact is the **received view in the Japanese literature** (Furushima 1939; Aono 1982), resting on individual village studies. Brown 2006 tests it at regional scale with GIS over 113 villages and concludes that flood and landslide frequency "must be seen as insufficient causes by themselves, even if scholars ultimately see them as necessary conditions," and that explanation must be sought "more widely… than the threat of natural calamity stressed heretofore." Coding `MC1=pooling` would enter a contested orthodoxy as data; coding a `HZ1` value would assert a correlation its only regional test failed to find. Both are held open rather than resolved.

**`CN1` is `.NA` and that is the most informative cell in the batch.** *Warichi* has no contribution stream and accumulates no fund; it reallocates an existing asset rather than assembling one. Every other form in this census collects something — the *avería* levy, general average's ex-post assessment, the 掛金 of the *tanomoshi* associations, the tontine subscription. This is where *warichi* is unlike all of them, and it is the structural reason the pooling category fits badly. `CN2`, `CN3` and `LS2` propagate `.NA` from it.

**Best-attested cell is `PY2=rotation-lot`**: the village kept 「鬮替帳」 (*kujikae chō*, Lot Exchange Notebooks), and in 1756–57 the sixteen shares were organised into nine lot-drawing groups (*kujigumi*).

**Two characteristics fit awkwardly and are flagged rather than forced.** `PY0`/`PY1` record a draw by lot irrespective of loss, which is right about the trigger and wrong about the structure: all members draw simultaneously at each redistribution, and there is no queue or turn as in every rotating form previously coded. And *hikikuji* — the right to withhold land from the redistribution, running from under 2 per cent of village land in 1756–57 to 14 per cent in 1818, exceeding the 5 per cent Kaga allowed, and "concentrated in a few hands, not universally shared" — has **no cell anywhere in the vocabulary**. An opt-out from the allocation mechanism is not representable. Recorded in the `PY2` note and in logbook 6.

**Analytical framing kept out of the data.** The vault argues that *warichi* spreads the *incidence* of an irreversible loss rather than reversing it, pairing it functionally with general average, and that Brown's hazard variable measures exposure where a persistence measure is wanted. None of that is coded. See vault notes on incidence-spreading and on the equality-versus-dispersion test.

## 2026-08-08 — ai (organizational_forms)

`nakai_fictive_household` coded across all 32 characteristics, `OF-0156`–`OF-0187`. **The first Japanese form in this dataset**, and the first coded from an English-language source in it. From D'Amico 2026 (*Enterprise & Society*, `10.1017/eso.2026.10131`), drawing on the Nakai Genzaemon archive.

**Why the form is worth the coding.** Tokugawa merchants exploited the fact that an *ie*'s name need not correspond to a real person, and built fictive households that held assets, contracted, and were registered as households while having no body behind them. Nakai Iwanosuke "founded" the Osaka store in 1806 aged five, died in 1807, and went on paying interest on the main house's prospective funds until 1845 — over 20,000 *ryō*. Clerks cited his youth on the 1827 *shūmon aratame-chō* and switched to sickness in 1829 and 1835.

**Two cells decide things.** `MG4=private-contract` — no charter, no grant. The authorities enforced private contracts but designated partnership accounts *nakama-goto* and non-adjudicable, so these entities arose **against a standing refusal**, not merely in the absence of enabling law. `CI3=0` — D'Amico states flatly that Tokugawa houses "failed to develop joint-stock equity finance or transferability in the absence of any real use for these features." Shares (*kuchi*) existed and were proportioned; they did not circulate.

**What is now visible in the matrix.** `MG4` has all four values occupied for the first time (state-charter, customary, religious, private-contract). `FP3 identity wrapper` gains its first `1` — every other coded form is `0`, so the fictive household is the census's only pure identity wrapper. And `AP1=1` beside `AP3=0` gives entity shielding without limited liability in a single form, which is the Hansmann–Kraakman asymmetry observed outside the European lineage.

**The near-neighbour is `waqf_khayri`, which was not expected.** On the WP2 five the two forms nearly coincide: `AP1=1/1`, `TS1=1/1`, `CI3=0/0`, `CI2=1/P`. They diverge on `LP1` (0 against P), `MG4` (religious against private-contract), and `FP3` (0 against 1). A Japanese commercial network and an Islamic pious endowment arriving at the same entity-continuity-without-transferable-claims profile, from unrelated ancestral conditions and opposite purposes, is a candidate independent-arrival pair and should be assessed as one rather than asserted.

**Coded conservatively where the source says it cannot support more.** `LP3` is `.NR` because D'Amico writes that partnership matters were non-adjudicable, "so few legal sources survive", and "how liability worked in the case of bankruptcy for fictive household entities remains a mystery". `LR1` is `low` on a single case (the 1839 Karasu dissolution of Ōta-ya Uhe). `AP4` is `P` at `low` with the waqf-shaped caveat live — an *ie* inherited generation after generation has no founder's estate in AP4's sense. `MG3` is `.NA`: `MG1=contract` leaves no membership whose entry could be restricted, which `check_dependence.py` caught after an initial `.NR`.

**Verification queue.** The Sōma 1783 clause — the manager's share "to be used as a bonus for hard work; however, losses will be divided by 19 shares" — carries both `LR6` and `CF2` and is terse enough to read either way. Check against Egashira before either cell is used.

## 2026-08-08 — ai (loss_mitigation_forms)

Five Japanese rotating associations coded, `LM-0104`–`LM-0163`. From twelve Japanese-language items on 頼母子講 / 無尽講 supplied by MS.

**The unit is the named association, not the form.** `ko_mochiyori_yutsu_1876`, `ko_hokoin_1832`, `ko_usui_bango_1822`, `ko_shijunin_1829`, `ko_daimanin_1828` — each backed by a surviving 規約書 or 仕法帳. The abstraction `ko_mujin` in the type vocabulary is left uncoded and superseded.

**Why not an ideal type.** The first attempt was to split by function — 親頼母子 / 社寺講 / 村方講 — on the model of the tontine split. It does not survive the sources. 碓井村伴吾講 (1822) allocates 初会入札、弐会目振鬮、終リ迄一会替リ, alternating bid and lot round by round to the twenty-fifth; 四拾人講 (1829) allocates 振鬮を以 alone across forty-one rounds; 大満院講 (1828) inherits 伴吾講's rules verbatim because 岡田伊左衛門 and 塩野久右衛門 drafted the 仕法 for both. Same district, one decade, overlapping drafters, different allocation rules. **The allocation rule is a drafting choice by named 銀預り, not a property of the tradition** — so no ideal type can carry it, and no sentence of the form "Japanese rotating associations allocate by lot/bid" is supportable.

**Why not a period split.** MS's instruction, and the sources bear it out from a second direction: 松永 1999 on 頸城郡 shows collateral entering the same village form between Kyōhō and Hōreki–Meiwa, driven by *widening geographic scope* and consequent 信用が不安 rather than by date. Period would have encoded a developmental sequence the sample cannot support — the tontine error.

**What the association-level unit bought.** `articulation` becomes load-bearing: most cells now rest on clauses quoted verbatim from a 仕法帳 rather than on a modern description. The emic vocabulary survives in `notes` — 実掛/空掛/からかけ, 花鬮 and 華鬮, 振鬮, 講元/講親 against 銀預り/世話方, 当選銀, 満会, 席掛.

**Not coded, deliberately.** 榊原 2014 (無尽講の経済的意味) is a three-person three-period model, not evidence about how any kō operated; coding structural characteristics from it would be coding a model. Its result belongs in the vault instead. `ko_daimanin_1828` is coded thinly on purpose — a near-copy of 伴吾講, so full coding would inflate *n* without adding a degree of freedom. 松永's 赤沢村茂右衛門頼母子 (文化4) and 三浦's 法隆寺五十貫頼母子 (室町) are read but not yet coded; together they would give a Muromachi-to-Bunka span on the collateral question. 加藤 1998 is unread (820k characters, 落札 ×54) and is the deepest source on bid mechanics.

**Historiographical caution carried into the codings.** 三浦 1959 argues medieval kō combine 営利的性格 and 相互扶助的性格 構造的に分ち難く, and that kō which were 実質的には収奪組織 coexisted with mutual-aid kō. The extractive reading is therefore the tradition's own structural feature, not a modern imposition by 渋谷/森/福山. Held open as a coding rather than resolved.

**Provenance.** All rows `coder=ai`, provisional. Sources are Japanese-language secondary works and primary-source editions; `source_lang=ja` throughout.

## 2026-08-07 — ai

`tontine` split into `tontine_en_1693` and `tontine_fr_royal`; `widows_fund_scotland` and `life_annuity_priced` added. `LM-0079`–`LM-0103`.

**Why the split.** The generic `tontine` row was entered with an explicit doubt recorded against it — that the label "probably spans pre- and post-actuarial forms, so one row may not hold it." It does not. The French royal series from 1689 divided subscribers into fourteen age classes carrying differentiated rates; the English tontine under the Million Act of 1693 did not differentiate by nominee age at all. Same mechanism, same decade, opposite values of `PR1`. One row cannot carry that, and smearing it would have destroyed the only result the form produces.

**What the split delivers.** `PR1` now has variance *inside* `MC1=pooling` at a fixed date, which is the falsification test `LM-0078` declared and could not run. It comes out against the sequence reading: pricing is orthogonal to mechanism, not a stage of it. This follows `CHARACTER-CODING.md`'s rule exactly — **add forms, not features** — and repeats the *asiento* pattern, where one new form settled in an afternoon what no amount of reasoning could.

**Why `widows_fund_scotland`.** Needed as the comparator, or `tontine_fr_royal PR1=1` asserts nothing. The Scottish fund is contemporaneous, also `MC1=pooling`, also priced ex ante — and `PY0=individual-indemnity` against the tontine's `individual-draw`. Pricing and payout direction therefore vary independently across the two, which is the discriminant-validity observation that `CHARACTER-CODING.md` test 4 says cannot be settled analytically. `MB3=compulsory` is coded `low` and unverified; if it holds, this is a compulsory pool *not* explained by non-excludability, which sharpens the open question standing against `averia_pool MB3`.

**Why `life_annuity_priced`.** The form that actually carries the pricing story — de Witt 1671 against the flat fourteen years' purchase, Hudde's Amsterdam registers, Halley 1693. Coded `MC1=spreading`, so it sits outside the pooling census and makes visible that the quantification episode belongs to a different mechanism than the one the tontine instantiates.

**Left alone deliberately.** `LM-0077` and `LM-0078` on the generic `tontine` type are **not** deleted or rewritten — schema and data growth here are additive, and the superseded coding should stay auditable, as with `cooperative_pooling_forms`. They are superseded by `LM-0079`–`LM-0093` and should be retired by the maintainer at review, not by the coder who replaced them. The `tontine` row in `vocabularies/loss_mitigation_type.csv` is likewise retained as a superordinate label.

**Not added.** `viatical` (out of period, though structurally identical to the tontine on `PY0` and worth the census if the argument runs to the present). `tontine_gb_1789` — the last major British issue, and the case that would test whether the English series itself moved to age classes before abandonment. Both parked here rather than coded from general knowledge.

**Provenance warning.** Every row is `coder=ai`, most carry `[verify]`, and the two figures the argument leans on — £108,100 tontine subscription against £118,506 in single-life annuities at fourteen years' purchase, and the French fourteen-class structure — come from secondary literature surveyed 2026-08-07, not from Exchequer records. Verify against Weir (1989), Velde & Weir (1992) and Milevsky (2015) before any of it is analytically used.
