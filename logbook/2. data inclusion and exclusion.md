# 2. Data inclusion and exclusion

> Record what enters the dataset and — more importantly — what is deliberately left out, and why.

## 2026-08-07 — ai

`tontine` split into `tontine_en_1693` and `tontine_fr_royal`; `widows_fund_scotland` and `life_annuity_priced` added. `LM-0079`–`LM-0103`.

**Why the split.** The generic `tontine` row was entered with an explicit doubt recorded against it — that the label "probably spans pre- and post-actuarial forms, so one row may not hold it." It does not. The French royal series from 1689 divided subscribers into fourteen age classes carrying differentiated rates; the English tontine under the Million Act of 1693 did not differentiate by nominee age at all. Same mechanism, same decade, opposite values of `PR1`. One row cannot carry that, and smearing it would have destroyed the only result the form produces.

**What the split delivers.** `PR1` now has variance *inside* `MC1=pooling` at a fixed date, which is the falsification test `LM-0078` declared and could not run. It comes out against the sequence reading: pricing is orthogonal to mechanism, not a stage of it. This follows `CHARACTER-CODING.md`'s rule exactly — **add forms, not features** — and repeats the *asiento* pattern, where one new form settled in an afternoon what no amount of reasoning could.

**Why `widows_fund_scotland`.** Needed as the comparator, or `tontine_fr_royal PR1=1` asserts nothing. The Scottish fund is contemporaneous, also `MC1=pooling`, also priced ex ante — and `PY0=individual-indemnity` against the tontine's `individual-draw`. Pricing and payout direction therefore vary independently across the two, which is the discriminant-validity observation that `CHARACTER-CODING.md` test 4 says cannot be settled analytically. `MB3=compulsory` is coded `low` and unverified; if it holds, this is a compulsory pool *not* explained by non-excludability, which sharpens the open question standing against `averia_pool MB3`.

**Why `life_annuity_priced`.** The form that actually carries the pricing story — de Witt 1671 against the flat fourteen years' purchase, Hudde's Amsterdam registers, Halley 1693. Coded `MC1=spreading`, so it sits outside the pooling census and makes visible that the quantification episode belongs to a different mechanism than the one the tontine instantiates.

**Left alone deliberately.** `LM-0077` and `LM-0078` on the generic `tontine` type are **not** deleted or rewritten — schema and data growth here are additive, and the superseded coding should stay auditable, as with `cooperative_pooling_forms`. They are superseded by `LM-0079`–`LM-0093` and should be retired by the maintainer at review, not by the coder who replaced them. The `tontine` row in `vocabularies/loss_mitigation_type.csv` is likewise retained as a superordinate label.

**Not added.** `viatical` (out of period, though structurally identical to the tontine on `PY0` and worth the census if the argument runs to the present). `tontine_gb_1789` — the last major British issue, and the case that would test whether the English series itself moved to age classes before abandonment. Both parked here rather than coded from general knowledge.

**Provenance warning.** Every row is `coder=ai`, most carry `[verify]`, and the two figures the argument leans on — £108,100 tontine subscription against £118,506 in single-life annuities at fourteen years' purchase, and the French fourteen-class structure — come from secondary literature surveyed 2026-08-07, not from Exchequer records. Verify against Weir (1989), Velde & Weir (1992) and Milevsky (2015) before any of it is analytically used.

## 2026-07-29 — ai

`organizational_forms` is seeded, not censused. Recorded from the design session; provisional, pending MS ratification.

**What is in.** Fully coded so far: `waqf_khayri` across all 31 characteristics; plus illustrative cells for `joint_stock`, `natie`, and `nacion_cofradia`. Every row is `coder=ai` with `source_ref=[verify]` where the citation is not yet pinned — provisional seed to verify before any analytical use.

**Split, not blurred.** The waqf is coded as two type rows, `waqf_khayri` (charitable) and `waqf_ahli` (family), because they diverge on purpose orientation (`FP1`), governance (`MG2`), and threat model (member-creditor exposure versus sultanic confiscation and forced inheritance). Only `khayri` is coded so far. General principle for this dataset: the unit is the form as ideal-typed in a stated period; a sharply divergent variant gets its own type row rather than a smeared single code.

**Census deferred, with a stated bias correction.** The exhaustive form list is a later pass. Latin/European forms to be drawn from Harris 2020 (commenda, fraterna, societas, VOC, chartered companies). Asian forms are to be added deliberately rather than as an afterthought, since they are underrepresented in that literature: Japanese (`ie`, `kabu_nakama` already; `ton_ya`, `ko_mujin`, za, kumi), Chinese (`huiguan`, `gongsi`, `piaohao`, qianzhuang, hang guilds), South Asian (`huf`, and the medieval merchant guilds Ayyavole and Manigramam), Islamic/Inner-Asian (`mudaraba`, `qirad`, `ortoq`). Candidates are parked in `vocabularies/organizational_form_type.csv`.

## 2026-07-19 — MS

`instrument_type: bill_note` (手形 / tegata, `vocabularies/instrument_type.csv`) is coded as a single category across the sample period, but the term's referent broadens over the Edo period: early tegata read as a simple promissory note, later ones shade toward a negotiable instrument closer to `remittance_bill`. Kept as one code for now rather than split, because the sample is too thin to fix a period boundary between the two senses. Revisit once enough dated instances accumulate to locate where the shift falls; until then, `date_iso` lets a reader stratify by period and judge for themselves.
