# 2. Data inclusion and exclusion

> Record what enters the dataset and — more importantly — what is deliberately left out, and why.

## 2026-08-29 (ii) — ai — `maona_chios` included; the 1347 Old Maona not coded

**`maona_chios`** (New Maona, 1362–1566), from Taviani 2022 and Balletto 1990. It separates from `maona_corsica` on `CI3` — Chios participants "can sell their shares to anyone they want" (Taviani 30) against Corsica's transfer "solo tra i partecipi o loro congiunti" (Petti Balbi 162) — which is why coding one `maona` row would have destroyed the observation. After the `LP2` revision the two agree on 27 of 32 and differ substantively on `CI3` and `CI4` alone.

**The 1347 Old Maona is a separate constitution and is deliberately not coded.** It differs from the New Maona on `TS2` (no twelve-year term), `MG3` (no *populares* requirement, no floor of twelve) and `CI3`. On the standing splitting rule it is a third maona row. **Whether the census wants three maone is a diversity-budget question, not a coding one, and it is not answered here.**

**`TS3=1`, decided rather than defaulted.** The Commune's power to redeem every *locum* at a fixed 75 lire, in named windows, is coded 1 — the first revocable form in this dataset and the first instance of a value that had none. The windows constrain when, not whether. The losing reading (`P`, on "at will" requiring continuous availability) is recorded in the cell. **`TS3` does not record which party holds the power**: here it is the grantor's, and a founder-revocable *waqf* would read identically.


## 2026-08-29 — ai — three inclusions, two splits, and one form deliberately not coded

**Decisions by MS.** Entity-census batch: `casa_san_giorgio`, `maona_corsica`, and the VOC as three dated rows.

**`casa_san_giorgio` included.** The census's canonical reference form was absent while the vault argued about it in 33 notes; the coding put Harris's exclusion of San Giorgio from the possible models of the corporation (reported at Taviani 2022, 12) to a test it had never had.

**`voc` split three ways: `voc_1602` (1602–1611), `voc_1612` (1612–1622), `voc_1623` (1623–1799).** On the standing rule that a type spanning both values of a characteristic must be split. `voc` as "1602 onward" spanned `TS2` and `CI2` at the 1612 transition to permanent capital, and `AP3`, `LR1`, `CF1` and `CF2` at the 1623 rejection of directors' personal liability. `P` was not available as a way round it: `P` is structural half-presence and never "changed over time". **The split immediately produced a result a single row could not have carried** — `voc_1602` and `voc_1612` share their `agent-loss-exposure` signature with `societas_maris`, and `voc_1623` with `asiento_averia`. See logbook 4.

**`cooccurrence_basis=same-institution` on all three VOC rows.** The VOC contributes ONE case to any *n*. Any count of the census must now say whether it counts rows or institutions, because after this batch those numbers differ: 23 rows over 21 institutions.

**`maona_corsica` included; `maona_chios` deliberately NOT coded.** They are different institutions and separate on a coded characteristic, so one row would span both values. Corsica permits transfer of quote "solo però tra i partecipi o loro congiunti" (Petti Balbi 1981, 162) — `CI3=P`; the Chios New Maona permits sale "to anyone they want" (Taviani 2022, 30) — `CI3=1`, with `MG3` differing too. Chios is separately codeable from Taviani's contract tables and is a second row, not an extension of this one.

**The maona's boundary question, settled from the source.** The 1378 cession is formally an enfeoffment — *in feudum rectum, nobile et gentile*, the six attributes of vassalic fidelity, the horse at Pentecost *in signum subiectionis*. Petti Balbi asks why and answers that the Republic masked what was "nello spirito e nella sostanza… una concessione d'appalto" to make it legible to "il mondo isolano ancora impregnato di feudalesimo" (151); the 1364 precedent was let openly to *socii appaltatores insule Corsice* (150). `MG4=state-charter` records the source of authority; the feudal idiom is the instrument's dress and lives in the cell note. **Note that `organizational_form_type.csv` has no `boundary_basis`/`boundary_confidence` columns** — that pair exists only on the loss-mitigation side, so this reasoning has nowhere structured to live.

### 長興寺村: three forms, and 小林 says so four times over

`ko_gotensama_1843`, `ko_daikokuya_1848` (already coded) and `ko_choko_1854` are three associations, not one association in three phases:

1. 小林 titles §5 三つの頼母子講 and numbers them 第一次 / 第二次 / 第三次 (299).
2. **They overlapped** — 天保14(1843)–嘉永5(1852) against 嘉永元(1848)–嘉永6(1853), which he calls 二重興行 (297). Phases of one thing do not run concurrently.
3. The convener changed: 殿様発起から大黒屋善兵衛の発起へと主宰者は代わっても (299).
4. Their assessments differ — the flat 家掛り on the landless runs 2匁8分 → 8分5厘 → 1匁2分 across the three (299) — and so do their meeting months: 御殿様 すべて11月 against 大黒屋's 4月 and 9月 (300).

**They are nonetheless not independent cases**, and the reason is stronger than "same village". 小林 at 300: the 大黒屋 銀集帳's contents and membership are 先行の「御殿様」頼母子講と殆ど同一. At 308: 同一人物が前後三つの頼母子講に名をつらねて. `co-occurrence_basis` is therefore raised to `same-persons` on all three (`ko_choko_1854` was `same-community`, which understated it), and `ko_gotensama_1843` now lists `ko_choko_1854` as well. No similarity or configurational claim may count the trio as three draws. This is the vault's *Forms are not independent observations when a village runs a portfolio* in a sharper form than the note anticipated: not three institutions segregated by medium, but three successive levies on one roll of about a hundred households.

## 2026-08-19 — ai (six forms put to the batch, two coded, four excluded)

The Zotero collection *HistorEE — huiguan, kongsi, HUF, confraternity, avarız, caravan*, 40 items, was read against the six uncoded rows named in the brief. **Two rows are coded; four are not, and the four are the more useful entry.** Rows `LM-0564`–`LM-0609`; proposal under `proposed/`, nothing committed.

### In: `avariz_fund`, renamed, `LM-0564`–`LM-0586`

**The row's old title was an error of fact and the coding could not proceed until it was fixed.** *"Avarız-hane fund (Ottoman communal levy)"* named two things neither of which is a mitigation institution. An **avârız hâne** is a fiscal accounting unit — three to ten real households counted as one, according to a district's wealth and whether its people were townsmen, villagers, migrants or nomads (Kuzucu 2016, 107, after Tabakoğlu and Sahillioğlu; Ünal 1987 works an avârız defteri of 1056/1646 on the same basis). Antep carried 373.5 of them at ten guruş each, pushed to thirteen as the count fell. The **avârız** is an extraordinary war tax to the imperial treasury: its payers get no pool.

What exists is the **mahalle or village avarız sandığı**, and the row now names it. Kars 2020, from İstanbul Şer'iyye Sicilleri nrs 3, 10, 12, 18, 20, 22 across 1618–1697, with 36 funds tabulated: *"Bu vakıflar mahalle halkının katkılarıyla kurulmuş ve çeşitli vesilelerle yapılan bağışlarla zenginleştirilmiştir"* — established with the contributions of the quarter's people and enriched by donations on various occasions; and *"her mahalle paylarına düşen avarız vergisi borcu için ortak bir bütçeyle avarız vakfı kurmaktaydı"* — each quarter founded one with a common budget for the debt falling to its share. Gürsoy 2019, from the Evkaf Muhasebeciliği court 1491–1828, gives the aggregation directly: *"Aynı mahallenin ihtiyaçları için birden fazla vakfedilmiş vakıflar avarız sandıklarında toplanarak gereken yerlerde kullanılmak için fon oluşturmuşlardır"* — several waqfs for one quarter's needs gathered into the chest to form a fund. 109 waqfs in that court, 38 of them avarız, the count rising some ninety per cent between the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

**This is the split candidate the census already asked for.** `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme`'s key_source records it as *"NOT YET EVIDENCED"* — Küçük noted avarız funds were capitalised either by wealthy donors or as an aid fund the inhabitants formed among themselves, and that the second variant would take `MC1=pooling` and needs its own row when a case is found. Two register series now supply it, and the row is not a duplicate: it separates from Kırkçeşme **on substantive values, not via `.NA`** — `MC1` pooling against allocation, `GV1` collective against `.NR`, `MB3` voluntary against `.NA`, `CN1` `.NR` against `.NA`. Kırkçeşme is one endowment of 6,280 kuruş with a salaried mütevelli and no contribution stream at all; the sandık accretes endowments for three centuries under trustees the residents elect.

**Independence is partial and is stated on the row.** Kars and Gürsoy work different registers and different jurisdictions but one city. Kıvrım 2019 on the 17c Ayntab funds would give a second province and is metadata-only in the library, with no attachment.

### Out: `huiguan` — no source in the batch treats it as loss mitigation

Every Chinese-tradition item was checked for burial, funeral, coffin, cemetery, almshouse, sickness, widow, orphan and relief. **Hamilton 1977** on the Swatow Opium Guild trial (23 pp, read in full) is about restraint of trade and returns zero hits on all of them. **Zelin 2019**, the source behind `shenhui_gu_alloc`, mentions native-place associations once, in n. 7, pointing to Rowe 1984 *Hankow* for *"the provision of communal social services"* — and Rowe is not in the library. **Wu 2025** uses native-place associations (*hanghui*) purely as the piaohao's punishment and information mechanism. **Wei 2009** and **Mokyr & Tabellini 2023** are general and secondary.

**The one relief fact in the batch belongs to a different body.** Lin Yu-ju (in Zelin ed. 2015, ch. 1, p. 20) records the Quan Jiao and Xia Jiao of Lugang jointly endowing a charitable burial ground (*yizhong*) at Jingyiyuan in 1775, and funding temple repairs and a free ferry — a **jiao**, and a collective good for the town rather than an indemnity to a member. Lin's chapter is in fact built on Chiu Peng-sheng's demonstration that *"the former practice of deeming jiao analogous to huiguan and gongsuo is incorrect"*, so the fact cannot be carried across.

**Also note the row's title asserts its own conclusion.** "Native-place association mutual fund" presupposes the fund this census would code, and no source here shows one.

### Out: `gongsi` — a capital vehicle, and a homonym

The batch's only treatment is one background paragraph, Kwee Hui Kian in Zelin ed. 2015, ch. 4, p. 79: the gongsi and other socio-religious organisations *"allowed for the pooling of capital and labour, mutual loans at low interest rates and a platform for the settlement of internal disputes among people of the same surname as well as home village and/or district."* Capital, credit and a tribunal. No hazard pooled and no loss met; her chapter contains no instance of insurance, death benefit, burial or indemnity. The specialist literature she cites — Trocki on the Ngee Heng kongsi, Yuan Bingling's *Chinese Democracies: A Study of the Kongsis of West Borneo*, Wang Tai Peng — is absent from the library.

Second problem: **gongsi is the ordinary modern Chinese word for "company"**, and in this batch it appears in that sense only, in publisher names in Zelin 2019 nn. 16–17. A census row keyed on the bare term will collect the wrong things.

**The question put to the batch — one form or two — gets this answer.** They are not one form; a native-place association in a treaty port and a shared-capital mining venture in Borneo differ on every dimension the sources describe. But the question cannot be settled *on this census's grounds*, because neither is documented here as loss mitigation at all.

### Out, and recommended for removal: `huf`

The BOUNDARY DOUBT already on the row — *"arguably a capital-vehicle rather than a hazard-pool; retained here provisionally, revisit once coded"* — is confirmed, and the review is short because there is nothing to review. **The only South Asian item in the collection is Wilson 1855**, a colonial revenue and judicial lexicon. Its fifty-odd *coparcenary* entries are about coparcenary **villages** — shares in land-revenue tenure. No *Mitakshara* entry; one incidental *Dayabhaga*. *Kartā* is glossed in a single line, *"the active or managing member of a family"*, and printed by the OCR as "KartI, KarttA". Nothing on liability, on the manager's exposure, on partition, on what the family bears when a venture fails.

### Out: `caravan_partnership` — and the obstacle is readability, not judgement

### Two candidates found, not coded, not in scope

**Ottoman `maktu`.** Papastamatiou 2010, 292 (18 pp, readable): the practical value of maktu is *"the collective responsibility which counterbalances the financial embarrassment or insolvency of some members of the community with the proportionate distribution of the taxes to the rest of them"*. That is pooling of an insolvency hazard among the exposed, pro rata, at community level — and it is **why** a quarter needs an avarız sandığı. Compulsory, locality-based, fixed lump assessment. Not in the vocabulary. Looks codeable.

**Korean `shijŏn` / `tojung` guilds.** Zelin ed. 2015, ch. 2, p. 43: at weddings, funerals and memorial services *"the tojung might give cash or lend money at a low interest rate"*, aid *"partly conducted by the guild itself, and sometimes managed by kye"* — with the author's own caution that mutual aid *"was not the main reason for creating guilds, but an additional function that arose due to the creation of the guild"*. A separate tradition and a separate row if pursued.

### In: `societas_maris`, on exactly the eleven characteristics `commenda` already carries

The code **did not exist** in `organizational_form_type.csv` — only in `loss_mitigation_type.csv` — so this is a form addition, not the filling of a blank. `mudaraba` did exist, with zero rows; see below.

**Why eleven and not thirty-two.** `commenda` is coded on `TS2`, `AP3`, `LR1`, `LR2`, `LR3`, `LR4`, `LR5`, `CF1`, `CF2`, `CF3`, `LR6` and nothing else. **Source.** van Doosselaere 2009, 64–68 — the Genoese notarial cartularies, 6,764 *commenda* ties 1154–1315, 93 per cent of all coded maritime ties. The discriminating statement is at 65, with the two liability rules side by side, and the contribution ratio is customary at one third to two thirds.

**The pair separates on two cells of eleven.** `LR6` (`symmetric` against `upside-only`) and `CF1` (`P` against `1`). It agrees on `TS2`, `AP3`, `LR1`, `LR2`, `LR3`, `LR4`, `LR5`, `CF2` and `CF3`. What that does to the `agent-loss-exposure` dependence group is the substantive result and is in logbook 4 (2026-08-18 (ii)).

**`CF1=P` deserves its own line** because it is the one separation outside the WP1 component sets and it is a `none`-component descriptive cell. The investor supplies capital only; the traveller supplies capital *and* labour. The capital–labour asymmetry is therefore half present — one-sided in the labour, two-sided in the capital — which is what `P` records and is not a hedge.

### Out: `mudaraba`, and this is a determination, not an omission

Left uncoded, on the model of `ortoq_alloc` in `loss_mitigation_type.csv`, with the reason on the type row. **`Mudārabah` is the Ḥanafī name for the institution this dataset codes as `qirad`.** Ramli 2018, 97, on al-Sarakhsī: *muqāraḍa* is used synonymously with *muḍāraba*, "the Malikites, Shafiʿites and Hanbalites unanimously use a different term i.e. *qirāḍ*", and "in general, be it *muḍārabah*, or *muqāraḍah* or *qirāḍ*, it is by definition a commercial association whereby an investor or capital provider (*rabb al-māl*) entrusts some amount of capital to an agent". Coding both rows would enter one institution twice and produce agreement on all thirty-two cells by construction.

This is the third time in two days that a census row has turned out to be a **name** rather than a form — after `commenda_alloc`/`qirad_alloc` (identical on the loss facet) and the Ragusan *collegantia* (a unilateral commenda wearing a bilateral commenda's name). At p. 65 the two liability rules stand side by side as invariable terms of the standard contract:

> In a unilateral commenda, the investor collected three-fourths of the net proceeds and **bore all liability for loss**, while the traveler received only one-fourth of the net and bore no capital risk. ## 2026-08-17 — ai (blind coding: Winkelman's Amsterdam bodemerijen)

Read 46 of the 48 notarial acts in Winkelman (ed.) 1983, *Bronnen tot de geschiedenis van den Oostzeehandel*, RGP Grote Serie 184/185/186 — nrs 946–962, 1991–2005, 3227–3242. ### In: `bodemerij_amsterdam`, `LM-0512`–`LM-0524`

**One form, not several, and the reason is a missingness rule rather than a judgement of taste.**

The corpus varies on four axes that could each have carried a split. Pledge object: keel and gear, fractional parts, freight in its own right, goods and their returns, the borrower's whole estate, sureties. Pricing basis: a flat per-voyage percentage in 31 acts, a monthly rate in five, an absolute sum in one (no. 2002, 60 gld on 600, "maeckende tesamen 660 gld."). Duration: 30 single-voyage against six running by time with free navigation. Peril definition: a bare "op 't recht aventuyr van der zee" in most, an enumerated piracy clause in four, and in nos 3240 and 3242 a clause importing the whole custom of insurance — "alle anderen periculen, egeene uytgesondert, die een asseuradeur naer coustume van asseurantie is loopende", *haverije grosse* included.

That last group looked like the strongest candidate for a second form, because in it the lender bears **partial** loss pro rata ("penninck ponts gelijck") and takes pro rata of net salvage, where classic bottomry is all-or-nothing on the pledged thing. **It was rejected on the `.NR`/`0` rule.** The other 30 contracts do not deny proportional sharing; they are *silent* on partial loss. Splitting the corpus here would have coded that silence as a contrary value and manufactured a difference where the evidence shows only a difference in what was written down. `check_dependence.py` makes the same point in its own idiom when it marks separations achieved via `.NA` as weak.

Two further facts confirm the single form. The six insurance-referenced and piracy-clause acts cluster on **three parties** — Abraham de Velaer as lender, Marten Corsen as borrower, Dirck Woutersen and the pair Breugel & Smith — and on **eighteen months**, July 1618 to September 1621. That is a drafting habit of particular counterparties, not an institution. And the notary calls every one of them `contract van bodemerij`, in a register that also holds the plain ones.

### Naming and dating: Amsterdam, 1601–1621, and not Hanseatic

**The row is named for what was actually read.** The corpus is the practice of two Amsterdam notarial offices — Jan Franssen Bruyningh and Jacob Meerhout, with Epo Stoltenburch appearing once by inserted copy — over twenty years. Calling it Hanseatic *Bodmerei* or a northern European form would assert a geographic reach the evidence does not carry, and **Frankot is the reason that assertion is not available cheaply**. Her case is that Lübeck, Reval, Danzig, Kampen and Aberdeen used differing law in court despite holding overlapping written compilations, so a shared text never licenses an inference to shared practice. That argument is *methodological* here and nothing more: neither Frankot 2007 nor Frankot 2010 mentions bodemerij, bottomry or the sea loan at all, and it must not be cited as though it did. Jenks 2010 on Prussian Hanseatic trade finance points the same way from the other side — his instruments are *Wechsel* and *Darlehen* under a *Kreditverbot*, with no bodmerei in view.

The acts do reach beyond Amsterdam, and that is recorded rather than used. Money is taken up on bodemerij at La Rochelle (nos 955, 957, 959), Danzig (nos 960, 961, 1994, 1996) and Königsberg (nos 2004, 2005, 3238); borrowers come from Stralsund, Emden, Reval and Harlingen. **But every one of those instruments enters the record because an Amsterdam notary protested it.** The corpus is a window cut by Amsterdam procedure, and the sample is of Amsterdam enforcement, not of Baltic practice.

### `boundary_basis = documented-instance`, `boundary_confidence = high`

**What the value claims, and what it deliberately does not.** It claims that this row exists because a dated, localised body of transactional practice was read and coded from the acts themselves, in the way the `ko_*` rows and `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme` do. It does not claim a structural difference from the existing `bottomry` row, and that is not modesty — **it could not be assessed.** This coding was run blind and the `bottomry` codings were among the withheld material. The boundary against `bottomry` therefore stands open, and the maintainer should treat re-basing this row to `structural-difference` (or merging it into `bottomry`) as the first sighted decision after the blind is lifted.

The boundary against `sea_loan` **can** be stated from the sources and is not the same question. `sea_loan` is separated on `contemporary-terminology`; what separates `bodemerij_amsterdam` from it is a **constitutive pledge named in every single act** — "op kielscheeps ende 't gereetschap van dien", or on named goods — the pledge that the word *bodem* denotes. No. 3236 goes further and invokes a customary law for it by name: the money runs "op 't recht avontuyr van der see, op kielscheeps ende 't gereetschap **naer recht ende costume van bodemerije**". A contemporary Dutch legal form with its own custom and its own instrument, the *bodemerijebrief*, is not the undifferentiated *foenus nauticum*.

### Out: nothing, but three limits on the sample belong on the record

**The two missing acts are not random.** Nos 946 and 947 are the first two of the earliest run; the loss truncates the corpus at its early end, so any statement about change between 1601 and 1621 begins one contract late.

**The 150 contracts Winkelman counts are not the population of Amsterdam bodemerij.** He says so himself: much bodemerij was raised without a notary, and abroad the master could raise it under the *Plakkaat* of 1563 art. 19 without any Amsterdam instrument at all. He also gives the reason the notarial series is thin — the *partenrederij*, the free advance on freight, and marine insurance at roughly half the opgeld were all competing with it. **The survival is of the expensive, contested and formal cases.**

**Winkelman is a publisher's transcription, not the manuscript.** Every reading here is at one remove, the OCR of the printed page adds a second, and the edition's own apparatus carries at least one gap in the substance — at no. 3240 the percentage is simply absent, and the editor notes it: "Het percentage niet vermeld; blijkbaar overgeslagen." That cell is therefore not coded from that act.

`warichi_iwade` coded on 18 characteristics, `LM-0178`–`LM-0195`, from Brown 2006 and Brown 2011. Iwade village, Echigo — periodic reallocation of cultivation rights by drawn lot.

**`MC1`, `HZ1` and `HZ2` are held at `.NR` on purpose, and this is the substantive decision.** The reading of *warichi* as spreading flood impact is the **received view in the Japanese literature** (Furushima 1939; Aono 1982), resting on individual village studies. Brown 2006 tests it at regional scale with GIS over 113 villages and concludes that flood and landslide frequency "must be seen as insufficient causes by themselves, even if scholars ultimately see them as necessary conditions," and that explanation must be sought "more widely… than the threat of natural calamity stressed heretofore." Coding `MC1=pooling` would enter a contested orthodoxy as data; coding a `HZ1` value would assert a correlation its only regional test failed to find. Both are held open rather than resolved.

**`CN1` is `.NA` and that is the most informative cell in the batch.** *Warichi* has no contribution stream and accumulates no fund; it reallocates an existing asset rather than assembling one. Every other form in this census collects something — the *avería* levy, general average's ex-post assessment, the 掛金 of the *tanomoshi* associations, the tontine subscription. This is where *warichi* is unlike all of them, and it is the structural reason the pooling category fits badly. `CN2`, `CN3` and `LS2` propagate `.NA` from it.

**Best-attested cell is `PY2=rotation-lot`**: the village kept 「鬮替帳」 (*kujikae chō*, Lot Exchange Notebooks), and in 1756–57 the sixteen shares were organised into nine lot-drawing groups (*kujigumi*).

**Two characteristics fit awkwardly and are flagged rather than forced.** `PY0`/`PY1` record a draw by lot irrespective of loss, which is right about the trigger and wrong about the structure: all members draw simultaneously at each redistribution, and there is no queue or turn as in every rotating form previously coded. And *hikikuji* — the right to withhold land from the redistribution, running from under 2 per cent of village land in 1756–57 to 14 per cent in 1818, exceeding the 5 per cent Kaga allowed, and "concentrated in a few hands, not universally shared" — has **no cell anywhere in the vocabulary**. An opt-out from the allocation mechanism is not representable. Recorded in the `PY2` note and in logbook 6.

**Analytical framing kept out of the data.** The vault argues that *warichi* spreads the *incidence* of an irreversible loss rather than reversing it, pairing it functionally with general average, and that Brown's hazard variable measures exposure where a persistence measure is wanted. None of that is coded. See vault notes on incidence-spreading and on the equality-versus-dispersion test.

## 2026-08-08 — ai (organizational_forms)

`nakai_fictive_household` coded across all 32 characteristics, `OF-0156`–`OF-0187`. **The first Japanese form in this dataset**, and the first coded from an English-language source in it. From D'Amico 2026 (*Enterprise & Society*, `10.1017/eso.2026.10131`), drawing on the Nakai Genzaemon archive.

**Why the form is worth the coding.** Tokugawa merchants exploited the fact that an *ie*'s name need not correspond to a real person, and built fictive households that held assets, contracted, and were registered as households while having no body behind them. Nakai Iwanosuke "founded" the Osaka store in 1806 aged five, died in 1807, and went on paying interest on the main house's prospective funds until 1845 — over 20,000 *ryō*. Clerks cited his youth on the 1827 *shūmon aratame-chō* and switched to sickness in 1829 and 1835.

**Two cells decide things.** `MG4=private-contract` — no charter, no grant. The authorities enforced private contracts but designated partnership accounts *nakama-goto* and non-adjudicable, so these entities arose **against a standing refusal**, not merely in the absence of enabling law. `CI3=0` — D'Amico states flatly that Tokugawa houses "failed to develop joint-stock equity finance or transferability in the absence of any real use for these features." Shares (*kuchi*) existed and were proportioned; they did not circulate.

**What is now visible in the matrix.** `MG4` has all four values occupied for the first time (state-charter, customary, religious, private-contract). `FP3 identity wrapper` gains its first `1` — every other coded form is `0`, so the fictive household is the census's only pure identity wrapper. And `AP1=1` beside `AP3=0` gives entity shielding without limited liability in a single form, which is the Hansmann–Kraakman asymmetry observed outside the European lineage.

**The near-neighbour is `waqf_khayri`, which was not expected.** On the WP2 five the two forms nearly coincide: `AP1=1/1`, `TS1=1/1`, `CI3=0/0`, `CI2=1/P`. They diverge on `LP1` (0 against P), `MG4` (religious against private-contract), and `FP3` (0 against 1). A Japanese commercial network and an Islamic pious endowment arriving at the same entity-continuity-without-transferable-claims profile, from unrelated ancestral conditions and opposite purposes, is a candidate independent-arrival pair and should be assessed as one rather than asserted.

**Coded conservatively where the source says it cannot support more.** `LP3` is `.NR` because D'Amico writes that partnership matters were non-adjudicable, "so few legal sources survive", and "how liability worked in the case of bankruptcy for fictive household entities remains a mystery". `LR1` is `low` on a single case (the 1839 Karasu dissolution of Ōta-ya Uhe). `AP4` is `P` at `low` with the waqf-shaped caveat live — an *ie* inherited generation after generation has no founder's estate in AP4's sense. `MG3` is `.NA`: `MG1=contract` leaves no membership whose entry could be restricted, which `check_dependence.py` caught after an initial `.NR`.

**Verification queue.** The Sōma 1783 clause — the manager's share "to be used as a bonus for hard work; however, losses will be divided by 19 shares" — carries both `LR6` and `CF2` and is terse enough to read either way. Check against Egashira before either cell is used.

## 2026-08-08 — ai (loss_mitigation_forms)

Five Japanese rotating associations coded, `LM-0104`–`LM-0163`. From twelve Japanese-language items on 頼母子講 / 無尽講 supplied by MS.

**The unit is the named association, not the form.** `ko_mochiyori_yutsu_1876`, `ko_hokoin_1832`, `ko_usui_bango_1822`, `ko_shijunin_1829`, `ko_daimanin_1828` — each backed by a surviving 規約書 or 仕法帳. The abstraction `ko_mujin` in the type vocabulary is left uncoded and superseded.

**Why not an ideal type.** The first attempt was to split by function — 親頼母子 / 社寺講 / 村方講 — on the model of the tontine split. It does not survive the sources. 碓井村伴吾講 (1822) allocates 初会入札、弐会目振鬮、終リ迄一会替リ, alternating bid and lot round by round to the twenty-fifth; 四拾人講 (1829) allocates 振鬮を以 alone across forty-one rounds; 大満院講 (1828) inherits 伴吾講's rules verbatim because 岡田伊左衛門 and 塩野久右衛門 drafted the 仕法 for both. Same district, one decade, overlapping drafters, different allocation rules. **The allocation rule is a drafting choice by named 銀預り, not a property of the tradition** — so no ideal type can carry it, and no sentence of the form "Japanese rotating associations allocate by lot/bid" is supportable.

**Why not a period split.** MS's instruction, and the sources bear it out from a second direction: 松永 1999 on 頸城郡 shows collateral entering the same village form between Kyōhō and Hōreki–Meiwa, driven by *widening geographic scope* and consequent 信用が不安 rather than by date. Period would have encoded a developmental sequence the sample cannot support — the tontine error.

**What the association-level unit bought.** `articulation` becomes load-bearing: most cells now rest on clauses quoted verbatim from a 仕法帳 rather than on a modern description. The emic vocabulary survives in `notes` — 実掛/空掛/からかけ, 花鬮 and 華鬮, 振鬮, 講元/講親 against 銀預り/世話方, 当選銀, 満会, 席掛.

**Not coded, deliberately.** 榊原 2014 (無尽講の経済的意味) is a three-person three-period model, not evidence about how any kō operated; coding structural characteristics from it would be coding a model. Its result belongs in the vault instead. `ko_daimanin_1828` is coded thinly on purpose — a near-copy of 伴吾講, so full coding would inflate *n* without adding a degree of freedom. 松永's 赤沢村茂右衛門頼母子 (文化4) and 三浦's 法隆寺五十貫頼母子 (室町) are read but not yet coded; together they would give a Muromachi-to-Bunka span on the collateral question. 加藤 1998 is unread (820k characters, 落札 ×54) and is the deepest source on bid mechanics.

**Historiographical caution carried into the codings.** 三浦 1959 argues medieval kō combine 営利的性格 and 相互扶助的性格 構造的に分ち難く, and that kō which were 実質的には収奪組織 coexisted with mutual-aid kō. The extractive reading is therefore the tradition's own structural feature, not a modern imposition by 渋谷/森/福山. Held open as a coding rather than resolved.

**Provenance.** All rows `coder=ai`, provisional. Sources are Japanese-language secondary works and primary-source editions; `source_lang=ja` throughout.

## 2026-08-07 — ai

`tontine` split into `tontine_en_1693` and `tontine_fr_royal`; `widows_fund_scotland` and `life_annuity_priced` added. `LM-0079`–`LM-0103`.

**Why the split.** The generic `tontine` row was entered with an explicit doubt recorded against it — that the label "probably spans pre- and post-actuarial forms, so one row may not hold it." It does not. The French royal series from 1689 divided subscribers into fourteen age classes carrying differentiated rates; the English tontine under the Million Act of 1693 did not differentiate by nominee age at all. Same mechanism, same decade, opposite values of `PR1`. One row cannot carry that, and smearing it would have destroyed the only result the form produces.

**What the split delivers.** `PR1` now has variance *inside* `MC1=pooling` at a fixed date, which is the falsification test `LM-0078` declared and could not run. It comes out against the sequence reading: pricing is orthogonal to mechanism, not a stage of it. This follows `CHARACTER-CODING.md`'s rule exactly — **add forms, not features** — and repeats the *asiento* pattern, where one new form settled in an afternoon what no amount of reasoning could.

**Why `widows_fund_scotland`.** Needed as the comparator, or `tontine_fr_royal PR1=1` asserts nothing. The Scottish fund is contemporaneous, also `MC1=pooling`, also priced ex ante — and `PY0=individual-indemnity` against the tontine's `individual-draw`. Pricing and payout direction therefore vary independently across the two, which is the discriminant-validity observation that `CHARACTER-CODING.md` test 4 says cannot be settled analytically. `MB3=compulsory` is coded `low` and unverified; if it holds, this is a compulsory pool *not* explained by non-excludability, which sharpens the open question standing against `averia_pool MB3`.

**Why `life_annuity_priced`.** The form that actually carries the pricing story — de Witt 1671 against the flat fourteen years' purchase, Hudde's Amsterdam registers, Halley 1693. Coded `MC1=spreading`, so it sits outside the pooling census and makes visible that the quantification episode belongs to a different mechanism than the one the tontine instantiates.

**Left alone deliberately.** `LM-0077` and `LM-0078` on the generic `tontine` type are **not** deleted or rewritten — schema and data growth here are additive, and the superseded coding should stay auditable, as with `cooperative_pooling_forms`. They are superseded by `LM-0079`–`LM-0093` and should be retired by the maintainer at review, not by the coder who replaced them. The `tontine` row in `vocabularies/loss_mitigation_type.csv` is likewise retained as a superordinate label.

**Not added.** `viatical` (out of period, though structurally identical to the tontine on `PY0` and worth the census if the argument runs to the present). `tontine_gb_1789` — the last major British issue, and the case that would test whether the English series itself moved to age classes before abandonment. Both parked here rather than coded from general knowledge.

**Provenance warning.** Every row is `coder=ai`, most carry `[verify]`, and the two figures the argument leans on — £108,100 tontine subscription against £118,506 in single-life annuities at fourteen years' purchase, and the French fourteen-class structure — come from secondary literature surveyed 2026-08-07, not from Exchequer records. Verify against Weir (1989), Velde & Weir (1992) and Milevsky (2015) before any of it is analytically used.
