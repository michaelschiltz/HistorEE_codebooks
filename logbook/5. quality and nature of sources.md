### But character fidelity is poor on transliterated terms, and it generates false negatives

This is the part worth carrying forward, and it is a new instance of an old rule.

In the Çizarça OCR, **`waqf` is rendered `wagf` 76 times, `wagfs` 61 times, `wagqf` 38 and `wags` 5,
against only 15 correct `waqf` and 17 `waqfs`.** A `q`→`g` substitution on roughly four occurrences in
five. **A coder grepping the OCR for `waqf` in the central article on cash waqfs would find a small
minority of them and could reasonably conclude the article was off-topic.** Likewise `kefil`, `rahn`
and `nema` return zero hits, which may mean the terms are absent or may mean they are mangled; the
text alone cannot distinguish the two.

> **On an OCR'd scan, an exact-string grep returning nothing is evidence about the OCR, not about the
> source.** Search with fuzzy patterns (`wa[qg]{1,2}f`, `ist[iı][gğq]l`), and never quote a
> transliterated term from OCR without checking it against the page image.

That is the 2026-08-16 rule — a negative conclusion about an institution is a claim about the
literature read — one level down, at the character.

One substantive by-product, since the fuzzy search had to be run anyway: **`avari*` occurs 121 times in
Çizakça 1995.** The 08-18 entry flagged that Bursa's own avarız-ı mahalle funds sit in his dataset with
capital figures across 1078–1239 AH; the text is now searchable and that series is reachable. It bears
directly on `avariz_fund`.

### Two false absences, and in neither case was the tool lying

Both are failures of *where I read*, not of what I read, and they cost most of a session.

**(i) A mirror that had stopped syncing.** `zotero-mcp` **reads** from Zotero's local HTTP API on
`localhost:23119` and **writes** to the Zotero web API. Those agree only while the desktop syncs. The
desktop's last sync was 2026-08-18 08:28:43 UTC; Zotero migrated its schema from 125 to 129 — the
Zotero 10 upgrade — at 09:33 UTC, about an hour later, and sync did not run again for thirty hours.
Everything written through the MCP in the interval was on the server and invisible locally. **I
concluded from this that the write tools were no-ops fabricating their confirmations, and that the
08-18 (ii) housekeeping below had never happened. Both conclusions were wrong** and were retracted
the same day, after MS synced and all of it appeared: `VABWM7NZ`, `B2GCWV23`, `4PI4SW9R`, `A2G93ZRX`,
the four re-filings into `3CGGDGFW` (30 items → 38), and the tag changes on `V4S222VJ`. **The
2026-08-18 (ii) entry stands as written and needs no correction.**

What settled it before the sync — and what should be used first next time — is that **Zotero marks
locally-created objects `version = 0`, `synced = 0` until they are pushed.** The library contained
zero such rows, across items and collections, while carrying 97 items dated 2026-08-16, 43 dated
08-17 and 5 dated 08-18. A tool writing locally cannot produce that; a tool writing to the server and
letting sync deliver produces exactly it.

Also worth recording so it is not re-diagnosed as loss: the database went from 128.6 MB to 45.9 MB
across the upgrade. That is the migration's `VACUUM`. Items 15,437 → 15,437, top-level 9,751 → 9,751,
collections 104 → 104, `3CGGDGFW` 30 → 30 either side.

**(ii) A checkpoint that was not the database.** Immediately after the sync the MCP returned the new
objects while a direct read of `zotero.sqlite` still showed the previous day's state, with the file's
mtime unmoved. Zotero runs SQLite in **WAL mode**, and 15.8 MB of sync had gone into
`zotero.sqlite-wal` without a checkpoint. The reads had been opening the file with `?immutable=1`,
which tells SQLite the file cannot change and **to ignore the WAL entirely**.

> **Never read `zotero.sqlite` with `immutable=1` while Zotero is running.** Check `zotero.sqlite-wal`
> first: if it is non-zero the main file is stale by that much. Copy the `.sqlite` *and* the `-wal`
> together and open the copy read-write so the log is replayed.

The earlier reads in the session were sound only because the WAL happened to be 0 bytes at the time.
That was luck, not method.

**The holdings rules now read:** an attachment count is not a source; a collection scope is not the
library; an unsynced mirror is not the library; and a checkpoint is not the library.

**A standing risk noticed on the way.** The Zotero data directory lives inside Google Drive
(`…/GoogleDrive-…/My Drive/Zotero`). The folder holds `zotero.sqlite.damaged`,
`zotero.sqlite-1.damaged`, a `zotero (1).sqlite.bak` conflict copy and some forty `.bak` files.
Zotero warns against cloud-syncing the data directory for exactly this reason.

| Pryor, 'Commenda… at Marseilles' | `WF9IKSJN` | "PDF, 1 attachment" | 44-page image scan, no text layer. OCR'd here. |

**The rule to add.** The brief already records two: a substring search returning nothing is evidence
about the query string; and a PDF in `ZotMoov` proves an *item* with an attachment, not a
*collection* membership. A third is now paid for:

> **An attachment count proves neither a PDF nor a text layer.** Zotero counts `linked_url`
> attachments — a saved landing page, a DOI resolver, a Semantic Scholar record — identically with a
> held file, and counts an image-only scan identically with a searchable one. Before a source is
> promised to a coding session, check the child's `contentType` **and** that page 1 returns text.

Cheap to automate: `zotero_get_item_children` gives the content type, and one
`zotero_read_pdf_pages(start_page=1)` distinguishes a scan from a text PDF. Six items, two calls.

### OCR: what worked, and the language limit

Held and Pryor are both image-only scans. Both were rasterised at 300 dpi greyscale and run through
Tesseract 5 with `--psm 1`, page by page, in the session sandbox: `pdftoppm -r 300 -gray -png`, then
`tesseract <page>.png <page> -l eng --psm 1`. Output quality on Held was high enough to read Latin
clause text embedded in English prose (*ad periculum … maris et gentis clarefactum*, *malo modo et
in sua culpa*, *salvos in terra*) and to preserve footnote numbering. Two limits:

1. **Only `eng` traineddata is installed in the sandbox.** Held's Croatian bibliography is mangled
   (diacritics dropped, `Cremošnik` → `Cremoinik`) and any source in Italian, Latin or German would
   need `ita`/`lat`/`deu` installed first. Zeno 1936 was staged and confirmed to have **no text
   layer at all** (57 characters across five pages) and was **not** OCR'd for that reason — an
   Italian document collection through an English model would produce citable-looking noise.
2. The running marginalia of the DIZBI repository watermark reproduces on every page as garbage.
   Harmless, but it means a naive word count understates the text.

**Method note for the next session: OCR of a held scan is a five-minute operation and should be
tried before an item is recorded as unavailable.** Both of the two scans in this batch yielded
usable text; one of them turned out to carry the batch.

## 2026-08-16 — ai — acquisition depth, and an OCR method

### The first pass was thin and the thinness produced a false conclusion

One Undermind deep search returned 37 papers; **3 had retrievable PDFs.** The medieval Mediterranean
— the period `bottomry` and `respondentia` are dated to — was represented by nothing readable, so the
three maritime forms were effectively coded from Roman law at one end and 18th-century Portugal at

read, until the transactional corpus has been reached.**

### Zeno 1936 — an OCR method and its limits

The scan (424 pp.) has no text layer; Zotero's index carried only the introduction. OCR'd with
`tesseract` at `ita+lat` via `ocrmypdf --force-ocr`, ~4.6 s/page. Recovered 189 documents split on
roman-numeral headings, 91 of them carrying a loan or *risicum* clause.

Limits, recorded so the counts are not over-read: OCR of a 1936 scan; document splitting is
heuristic; every pledge hit was audited by hand and false positives removed (renunciation clauses
caught by a pledge pattern; one apparent ship-and-goods pledge that was the phrase "loaded in the
ship" inside a goods pledge). Sound to within a document or two, not to the unit.

Metadata verified from the title page: Riniero Zeno, *Documenti per la storia del diritto marittimo
nei secoli XIII e XIV*, S. Lattes & C. Editori, Torino, 1936, in *Documenti e Studi per la Storia del

## 2026-07-19 — MS

`money_exchange` (両替 / ryōgae, e.g. `mon_to_monme_via_board_rate` on `CR-0005`) conversions are priced from the posted ryōgae board rate for the transaction's date, not a modern reference table. The board rate is itself a primary source: its own folio/call number is recorded in `source_ref` alongside the transaction it prices, so the rate is as auditable as the transaction it converts. Confidence on these rows reflects the board rate's own legibility, independently of the transaction entry's.
