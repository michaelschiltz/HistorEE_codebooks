## 2026-08-18 (ii) — ai (the entity-side test of (v), and a correction to it)

`societas_maris` coded in `organizational_forms`, `OF-0283`–`OF-0293`, precisely to test what the
morning's entry asserted.

### Correction to (v) above

That entry said the unilateral/bilateral *commenda* pair is one "`organizational_forms` would have
every reason to merge … [and which] separates cleanly here". **The second half is right and the
first half is wrong, and the coding is what showed it.** `organizational_forms` separates the pair
too — on `LR6 coupling symmetry`, and on `CF1`. What the entry should have said, and what the
evidence supports, is narrower and better:

> The historiography merges the pair on the **payout**. Both datasets separate it on the **loss**.
> They separate it in different vocabularies — `RB1`/`RB2` name who bears which loss, `LR6` names
> the direction of the agent's exposure — but it is **one fact seen twice, not two confirmations**,
> and no comparative claim may count it as two.

The facet-relative point survives intact; the claim that the entity dataset would merge them does
not. Worth recording as a small caution: the morning's entry inferred what `organizational_forms`
would do from what it *is for*, rather than from what it codes. Eight of its thirty-two
characteristics are WP1 loss-side, so "the entity dataset" is not a facet.

### The result: `CF2` and `LR6` separate on substantive values for the first time

The `agent-loss-exposure` group is `{AP3, CF2, LR1, LR6}`, asserted on the argument that for
bilateral capital–labour contracts all four record one fact — the agent is a debtor. `asiento_averia`
broke it once, and `bazacle_mill` again, but **both of those have `CF2=.NA`**: they are multilateral
forms with no capital–labour asymmetry at all, so `CF2` separated from the rest only through a
missing token, which `check_dependence.py` correctly rates as weak — "inapplicability is a fact
about scope, not evidence that two characteristics vary independently."

`societas_maris` produces a signature that did not previously exist:

```
{AP3: 1, CF2: 0, LR1: limited, LR6: symmetric}   <- societas_maris
{AP3: 1, CF2: 0, LR1: limited, LR6: upside-only} <- commenda, ortoq_equity, qirad
```

**Three of the four members held identical while the fourth moves**, on substantive values, inside a
single contract family. `CF2=0` now occurs with `LR6` in both `symmetric` and `upside-only`.

This is the cleanest instance of `CHARACTER-CODING.md` test 4 the repo has. The *asiento* separated
the group by being a different kind of form — multilateral, no capital-labour asymmetry — which
always leaves the reply that the separation is an artefact of scope. This one holds the form type,
the tradition, the century, the city, the notaries and the contract's own drafting constant and
varies one thing: whether the traveller put in a third. **`LR6` is not determined by `CF2`, and the
reason is exactly the reason `LR6` was added** — a call bounded below at zero and a bounded but
two-sided exposure are different objects, and `CF2` cannot see the difference because it asks about
*the principal's* capital, not the agent's.

Note the direction, because it is the opposite of the *asiento*'s. There, `LR1=limited` was shown to
occur with two values of `LR6`. Here, the agent's own capital contribution moves `LR6` while leaving
`AP3`, `LR1` and `CF2` untouched. The group has now been separated from two independent directions
and should be treated as a reporting convenience, not as one degree of freedom.

### Postscript, same day: the group's collinearity was partly the coding

Written after `AP3` and `LR1` were given an explicit locator and recoded — logbook 1,
2026-08-18 (ii). It bears on the result above and should be read with it.

The `agent-loss-exposure` group was asserted on the argument that `AP3`, `CF2`, `LR1` and `LR6` all
record one fact for bilateral capital–labour contracts. `societas_maris` separated `LR6` from the
other three on substantive values. The `AP3` repair then separated `AP3` from `CF2` as well — and
that second separation is of a different kind, because it did not come from a new form. It came from
discovering that `AP3` had been answering `CF2`'s question on four rows, so that the perfect
inversion `AP3=1 ↔ CF2=0` was an artefact.

Worth keeping the two apart when this is written up. **One is evidence about the world; the other is
evidence about the instrument.** A dependence claim can fail either way, and only the first kind is
a finding about institutions. `CHARACTER-CODING.md` §4 says discriminant validity "cannot be settled
analytically" and needs cases where the constructs come apart — true, and this shows the corollary:
a group that *looks* collinear may be collinear only in the coding, and no number of additional
cases will reveal that. It took reading the characteristic's own definition against its notes.

On the corrected data `AP3` has **zero variance** across the bilateral family — every coded agent is
unshielded from venture creditors — so within that family it now carries no discriminating
information at all. It earns its place from the entity forms, where `asiento_averia` reads `1`,
`bazacle_mill` `P` and `nakai_fictive_household` `0`.

### `CF3=bilateral` is a collision waiting to happen

`CF3 principal count` takes `bilateral|multilateral` and means **how many principals**. The commenda
literature's "bilateral commenda" — van Doosselaere 2009, 65; Held 2025, 7 — means **both parties
invest**, and says nothing about how many they are. Both senses happen to be true of
`societas_maris` and the first is true of `commenda`, so the collision is invisible today.

It will not stay invisible. Multilateral commendae are well attested: "a single traveling party
could pool together goods from numerous investing parties" (van Doosselaere 2009, 66), and of the
150 contracts for the *Saint Esprit*'s 1248 voyage, 132 were *commendae*, one merchant holding
thirteen with eleven different investors (Harris 2007, 12 n.17, after Berlow 1979). Such a row would
read `CF3=multilateral` and would still be a **unilateral** commenda in the literature's sense.

No change proposed to the value set — renaming a state is not additive and the values are correct
for the question `CF3` asks. What is needed is a sentence in `CF3`'s definition saying which
question it asks and which it does not. Flagged rather than done, per the rule about characteristics.

### Two things checked and found not to be findings

- **Whether `societas_maris` separates from `commenda` on `CF2`.** It does not, and that is the
  point: the traveller bears his own third and none of the investor's two thirds, which is what
  `CF2` asks about. A coder reading "the traveller bears loss" and coding `CF2=1` would have
  destroyed the result above.
- **Whether `LR3` should be `P`.** Profit is split equally and loss pro rata to capital, so the
  traveller takes half the upside and a third of the downside. `LR3` asks whether returns are shared
  rather than fixed; they are, on both sides. The asymmetry is real and is in the note — and it is
  the mirror of the *ʿisqa*'s labour-compensation asymmetry, reached **without** a usury constraint,
  which is evidence that *ribbit* is not what produced that solution.

## 2026-08-18 — ai (what four allocation forms did to the sort key, and what they did to `RB4`)

Blind coding of `commenda_alloc`, `qirad_alloc`, `isqa_alloc`, `societas_maris`; `LM-0527`–`LM-0563`.
Four results, in descending order of how much they should change what the census is used for.

### (i) `RB4` has variance for the first time, and a retirement case is withdrawn

`isqa_alloc RB4=0`. Before this batch `RB4 discharge on total loss` read `1` for `sea_loan`,
`bottomry`, `respondentia` and `bodemerij_amsterdam` and `.NA` for four others — **one observed
value across the whole census**. The `sea_loan LS3` note (`LM-0526`) drew the obvious conclusion:
"`RB4` still has one observed value across four maritime forms and its retirement case is
correspondingly stronger."

It now reads `1 / 1 / 0 / 1` across the four forms added here. The *ʿisqa* manager owns half the
advance as a debt (*pelga milveh*) and is a **paid** bailee on the other half, so the obligation is
not extinguished by the loss of the venture (Ackerman-Lieberman 2011, 662–663). **The retirement
case is withdrawn.**

This is `CHARACTER-CODING.md` test 4 running again and coming out the same way as the *asiento*:
discriminant validity "cannot be settled analytically", and one form settled it. Note what did the
settling — not a maritime form, not a European one, and not one anybody would have added *in order
to* test `RB4`.

Caveat that should travel with the result: `RB4` and `organizational_forms AP3` are perfectly
collinear across these three forms (`1/1/0` both ways). They are in different datasets and so in no
declared `dependence_group`, but a WP1-level comparison that reaches across both packages would be
counting one fact twice. Flagged here because nothing in either checker can see it.

### (ii) `MC1=allocation` doubled and lost its extension

Before: four forms — `sea_loan`, `bottomry`, `respondentia`, `avariz_vakfi_kirkcesme`. Of the three
with `RB1`/`RB2` coded, all three read `capital-provider / labour-provider`: the peril moves to the
lender, the market stays with the borrower. `allocation` and "the sea-loan family" were
extensionally almost the same set, so the sort key could not be distinguished from a name for that
family.

After: eight forms, and the four added all read `RB1` and `RB2` **together** —
`capital-provider/capital-provider` (*commenda*, *qirāḍ*) or `shared/shared` (*ʿisqa*, *societas
maris*). `MC1=allocation` now spans the entire range of the `RB1`/`RB2` contrast and **cannot
express it**. It sorts by the *destination* of the peril (named party / third party / membership)
and is blind to *which* peril and to whether it is divided.

The independence runs both ways, which is the discriminant-validity point rather than a complaint
about `MC1`: `capital-provider/capital-provider` now occurs at `MC1=allocation`
(`commenda_alloc`, `qirad_alloc`), at `MC1=spreading` (`bodemerij_amsterdam`) and at `MC1=.NR`
(`particular_average`). `MC1` does not determine `RB1`/`RB2` and `RB1`/`RB2` does not determine
`MC1`.

**No characteristic is proposed.** `RB1` and `RB2` already do the work; what has changed is only
that a claim of the form "these forms share a mechanism" now needs to say *which* level it runs at.
Recorded so that nobody reads `MC1=allocation` as a similarity claim.

Second, smaller point about the same value, and it is about the source rather than the data. Harris
uses "spreading" in a second, incompatible sense in his own 2007 paper: an investing party who
"split his investment among several traveling parties… would serve as a method of risk spreading"
(13), and Pryor 1984, 437 describes Alazacie Carcassonne's *commendae* as "widely distributed
against the possibility of loss". That is portfolio diversification, not `MC1`'s `spreading`
(transfer to an outsider for a premium). The census's sort key is named for a word its principal
source uses two ways. `MC1`'s definition already pins the intended sense; no change, but the
ambiguity should be named in any prose that cites Harris for the trichotomy.

### (iii) `RB1` and `RB2` are separately articulated only where they diverge — and which one gets the clause depends on the tradition's doctrinal problem

`articulation` came out asymmetric across all four rows and the pattern is not noise.

In the Latin material the **peril** is the articulated one. The Ragusan notarial formula is
*dicti solidi sunt ad periculum dicti [N.] maris et gentis clarefactum* (Held 2025, 20) — it names
the peril, names the bearer, and says nothing whatever about price movement. The market-loss bearer
has to be recovered by inference from the absence of a repayment obligation, so `RB2` is
`analyst-imposed`.

In the Islamic material the same thing happens by a different route. Al-Sarakhsī's rule is that
"losses incurred in the venture are the responsibility of the investor" (Ramli 2018, 95) —
undifferentiated — but the sunnaic warrant he cites, the ḥadīth of al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib,
conditions the capital on the *muḍārib* going "neither on sea, nor down the oasis, nor buy a live
animal". Three perils, named. Peril articulated, market inferred, again.

In the rabbinic material it **flips**. Maimonides makes the manager answerable for "half of all
**trading** losses" (Ackerman-Lieberman 2011, 663) — the market side is the articulated one — and
the peril side is left to bailment law and has to be reconstructed. The reason is doctrinal: *ribbit*
forces the Talmudic discussion onto the return on capital, whereas a *periculum* clause and a voyage
ḥadīth are about the voyage.

So `articulation` here is recording a property of the **doctrinal pressure on the tradition**, not
of the institution. That is a use of the field worth knowing about before anyone treats
`articulation` counts as a measure of how explicit a tradition was.

Corroboration that the `RB1`/`RB2` pair is not an analyst's invention imposed on the period: Hoover
1926, 1180–1182 on the Genoese notaries — "the care with which the risk of loss by maritime disaster
is differentiated from ordinary business risks, and the attention given by the notaries to the
proper incidence of the marine risk upon the different parties to the agreement". And González de
Lara 2002, 259 builds her model on exactly two risks, "both navigation and commercial risks".

### (iv) One device, three traditions, and the matrix has nowhere to put it

All three loss-allocation regimes make the capital-provider's assumption of the peril **conditional
on the agent staying inside a stipulated route envelope**, and shift the whole peril to the agent on
breach:

- Statute of Dubrovnik 1272, VII,50 — if the tractator leaves the Adriatic (*Culfum*) without the
  commendator's permission, *totum periculum* is his (Held 2025, 12).
- The ḥadīth of al-ʿAbbās in al-Sarakhsī — capital given on condition he not go to sea, "and if the
  *muḍārib* did so, he had to guarantee the capital due to the risks involved" (Ramli 2018, 97–98).
- Al-Nuwayrī's fourteenth-century formulary — the agent travels "wherever he wishes in Islamic lands
  **on safe routes**" (Ackerman-Lieberman 2011, 664).
- **Added after acquisition:** the Genoese standard contract. The traveller "swore to abide by
  certain instructions concerning the use of the money, such as **restrictions on destinations or
  goods transacted**" (van Doosselaere 2009, 65) — across 6,764 ties. Note that Genoa and the
  ḥadīth of al-ʿAbbās restrict the *same two things*: where he may go and what he may buy.

Latin statute, Ḥanafī ḥadīth-warrant, Mamlūk formulary and Genoese notarial practice — four corpora,
same device. Ackerman-Lieberman reads the
Islamic version in modern terms — freedom from liability "could actually encourage him to invest in
a suboptimally risky asset", the adverse-selection problem of a payoff bounded below — which is
precisely why the clause exists in all three.

**Not a proposed characteristic, and the reason matters.** A "risk-window condition" cell would be
`1` for every allocation form in the census including the whole sea-loan family, where the deviation
rule is the same fact. Discriminating power is free; this would buy none. What it *is* is evidence
that `RB1` is a **conditional** allocation everywhere in this family, so an unqualified
`RB1=capital-provider` overstates in every row that carries it. Carried in the notes.

### (v) The historiography collapses unilateral and bilateral commenda on the payout; they do not collapse on the loss

Added after van Doosselaere 2009 was acquired mid-session.

Three authorities say the two are one contract. Pryor 1977; Luzzatto 1961, 119; van Doosselaere
2009, 65 n.7 — "I have adopted Pryor's idea that unilateral and bilateral commenda are essentially
the same agreement", the choice between them being "strictly based on the travelers' basic economic
level."

**The argument is made on the payout, and on the payout it is airtight.** Unilateral: investor takes
¾ of net, traveller ¼. Bilateral: halves, with the traveller in for a third. Van Doosselaere's
arithmetic at 65 n.6 — the bilateral traveller's half is his ¼ labour share on the investor's ⅔
(= 1/6) plus the whole return on his own ⅓ — shows the labour compensation is *identical*. The
bilateral form is the unilateral form with the traveller's own capital bolted on.

**On the loss they are not identical, and van Doosselaere says so in the sentence before.** The
unilateral investor "bore all liability for loss" and the traveller "bore no capital risk"; in the
bilateral "the liability for loss was proportional to the respective initial investments." The
traveller's downside is 0 in one and ⅓ in the other.

So the collapse is a **facet-relative** collapse, and the facet on which it holds is the one the
literature happens to compute. `loss_mitigation_forms` computes the other one. This is the sharpest
vindication so far of splitting the two datasets: a form pair that `organizational_forms` would have
every reason to merge — same parties, same duration, same governance, same profit economics —
separates cleanly here, on `RB1`/`RB2`.

The rows are kept apart, with `boundary_basis=structural-difference` and the dissent recorded on the
type row and in every cell note. **A reader who wants them merged should be told which facet the
merger is being computed on.** Note also that this is a second instance of the pattern in (iii): the
literature's verdict tracks what its sources make easy to measure, not what the institutions did.

### (vi) A structural difference between the commenda and the qirad that no cell can hold

`commenda_alloc` and `qirad_alloc` return identical values in every coded cell and both type rows
carry `boundary_basis=contemporary-terminology`. That claim is about **the loss-allocation
characteristics** and must not be widened, because one real contrast is now documented and nothing
here holds it.

Van Doosselaere 2009, 68, reading Udovitch 1970, 190–6: in the *qirāḍ* "any proportional division of
net proceeds agreed to by the partners was deemed acceptable", the legal manuscripts contemplating
splits "from half-and-half to 1/20–19/20", so "the cost of capital among eastern Mediterranean
long-distance participants was a function of market conditions and, of course, of one's position in
the traders' network." In Genoa "room for bargaining was very limited": 4,860 ties, 1154–1265, across
dozens of destinations and sizes from under one Genoese pound to several thousand, and "only a
handful of contracts provided a payout different from the customary" split.

**Negotiated against customary.** It is a difference in how the price of capital is formed, not in
whether the peril is priced, so `PR1` cannot carry it — `PR1=0` on both, and correctly. Nor should a
characteristic be proposed for it: it is a WP2-side question about the cost of capital, this is a
WP1 loss census, and `CHARACTER-CODING.md` test 3 would ask whose category "bargaining range" is
before it asked anything else.

What it does do is qualify a boundary claim, so it is recorded on both type rows and here.

Incidental, and useful: **van Doosselaere is a readable route to Udovitch 1970's pp. 190–6**, which
the library does not hold. Not a substitute — the page range is reported, not quoted.

Two things checked and found not to be findings:

- **Whether the profit ratio prices the peril.** It does not track the peril; Held 2025, 19–20 has
  the Ragusan ratios departing from the statutory 2/3 in the commendator's disfavour, which he reads
  as an overabundance of capital seeking investment. `PR1=0` stands.
- **Whether `commenda_alloc` and `qirad_alloc` separate on `VF1`.** They do in the data
  (`documentary` against `.NR`) and they must not be read as doing so. Udovitch 1970 and Khalilieh
  2020 are not held with text; the `.NR` is a gap in the library. See logbook 5.

## 2026-08-17 — ai (what the Amsterdam acts say that the matrix cannot hold)

Five things came out of the bodemerij corpus that bear on the instrument rather than on the
form. **None of them is a proposal to add a characteristic.** Four are recorded here
because the matrix cannot hold them; the fifth is a well-formedness problem in an existing
pair, offered for the maintainer's decision and not repaired.

### (i) `RB1` and `RB2` use one role label for two different people

`RB1` is coded `capital-provider` and means the **lender**: he advances the money and loses
it if the ship is lost. `RB2` is coded `capital-provider` and means the **borrower**: the
opgeld is a fixed figure that does not move with the price the cargo fetches, so the market
loss stays with the owner of the goods.

Both codings are right and together they are unreadable. The two characteristics were
written for the commenda-shaped case, where one party brings capital and the other brings
labour, and in that case `capital-provider` picks out one person. **A secured loan has two
capital providers**, and the label silently switches referent between the two cells.

This is Sereno's incomplete character statement: the **locator** is missing. `RB1` and
`RB2` name a variable (which loss) and a state (who bears it) but never say *of what* the
role is predicated — of the venture, or of the advance. It is not the `LR2`/`LR6` case,
because the characteristic is not asking two questions; it is asking one question with an
underspecified subject.

**The repair, if one is wanted, is a locator in the definition, not a new characteristic.**
Something of the form "roles are predicated of the *venture*, so the party advancing money
against a pledge is `third-party` where he has no stake in the outcome beyond his advance"
would settle it — and would incidentally force a re-reading of this row's `RB1`, since on
that locator most of these lenders are outsiders and the value would become `third-party`,
collapsing the distinction from `marine_insurance` that `RB1` currently carries. **That is
exactly why the decision is the maintainer's and not mine.** Recorded, not acted on.

### (ii) The premium is paid only if the venture succeeds, and no `CN1` value says so

`CN1` asks when contributions fall due and offers `ex-ante-periodic`, `ex-ante-lump` and
`ex-post-assessment`, the last glossed in the vocabulary as the general-average pattern —
contribute *after the loss*.

Bodemerij inverts it. The opgeld is fixed before departure and falls due 8 to 16 days
**after safe arrival**, and it is not payable at all if the venture is lost, because
principal and opgeld are forfeited together. A premium contingent on success is neither
ex ante in its timing nor ex post on a loss.

**No row was written for `CN1`.** Forcing `ex-post-assessment` would have told a downstream
consumer reading the definition that this behaves like general average, which is the
opposite of the truth. This is the difference between marine insurance and bodemerij that
the matrix currently cannot see: the underwriter takes his premium whatever happens, the
bodemerij lender takes his only out of a venture that came home. Winkelman's Verwer
quotation is the contemporary awareness of it — the lender charges roughly double the
insurance premium, and Verwer's first reason is that he must reinsure.

### (iii) On deviation the risk stops but the whole opgeld is still owed — and the acts disagree about it

The protests supply what the contracts do not, exactly as expected of dispute records.
**Deviation, not shipwreck, is the whole subject of this litigation.** In no. 955 the master
sailed on from Danzig to Riga and the lender declares "dat hij geen voirder resicque oft
pericule begeert te loopen; alsoe naer recht ende coustume sijne penningen verschenen
sijn" — the risk-bearing terminates, the principal accelerates, and the ground given is
custom. No. 957 is the same shape with the rate named, 16 per cent, and the same phrase:
"gemerct hij door 't veranderen van de voorsz. reyse niet gehouden is eenige voirdere
risicque te loopen." No. 959 likewise.

No. 1995 writes the rule into the contract, and writes it hard: on deviation "sal de voorsz.
hooftsomme mettet opgelt van dien datelijck verscheenen weesen, zonder dat alsdan den
voorn. Hendrick Voet eenich vorder resicque zal loopen", the borrower bound to pay "de
voorsz. somme **mette volle bodemerije in 't geheel**". The full risk price, for a risk the
lender has stopped running.

**But three later acts write the opposite rule.** In no. 3227 if the master sails outside
the contract "sullen evenwel die voorsz. penningen op 't aventuyr van der zee als boven
blijven loopende", with the opgeld adjusted afterwards "tot seggen ende discretie van goede
luyden hen dies verstaende". Nos 3237 and 3241 do the same, no. 3241 promising the lender
"verbeteringe van opgelt" for the extra exposure. No. 2001 goes further and makes the
instrument renew itself: on the named deviation the first 17 per cent falls due when the
ship passes home waters, "**ende sullen alsdan de hooftpenningen opnieuw weder op
bodemerije loopen**" at a fresh 17 per cent for the next leg.

**Three regimes for one contingency, in one city, inside twenty years, all called
bodemerij.** This is the sharpest single result of the coding and there is no cell for it:
the matrix has no characteristic for what happens when the *conditioning event* of a
contingent obligation is altered by the obligor. It is not `RB4`, which asks only about
total loss. Recorded here rather than made into a feature — and it is a better argument for
Frankot's pluralism operating *within* a jurisdiction than anything the compilations show
between jurisdictions.

### (iv) Ship-pledged and goods-pledged bodemerij are one instrument in Amsterdam practice

`check_boundary`'s own docstring names `bottomry` and `respondentia` as the motivating case
and says the boundary between them waits on **notarial evidence**. This is notarial
evidence, and it does not support a contemporary distinction.

In this corpus the two are drafted by the same two notaries, entered in the same registers,
often within days of each other, under the same heading, with the same clause architecture
— consideration, "op 't recht aventuyr van der zee", the voyage description closing on "wel
heynder maer niet verder te seylen", the repayment interval, the opgeld, the *verbindende*
clause. **The only thing that changes is the noun in the pledge clause.** Nos 3232 and 3234
pledge salt and its returns; nos 3227 and 3230 pledge the keel; no. 956 pledges ship and
freight together; nos 3236 and 3237 pledge the freight first and the ship second. Nos 3232
and 3233 are the same borrower, the same lender and the same day, and no. 3233 is recorded
simply as "in aller vougen ende manieren als vooren verhaelt is".

No Dutch term in the corpus corresponds to *respondentia*. The type row for `respondentia`
already records Schuster calling it an English nominal subdivision, and its
`boundary_basis` is already `analyst-split`. **This corpus is independent confirmation
from practice**, and the maintainer may want to raise that row's `boundary_confidence` from
`medium` — a sighted decision, since the codings are withheld from this session.

### (v) A candidate form, deliberately not created: the floating bodemerij

Six acts (nos 962, 1999, 2003, 3229, 3231, 3239) have **no voyage at all**. No. 3231 lets
the ship sail "Oost, West, Suyden ende Noorden, naer alle ende ygelijcke alsulcke plaetsen,
havenen ende reeden als hem comparant believen sal"; no. 962 runs a calendar year; no. 1999
runs by the month with early repayment at the borrower's option and opgeld pro rata. The
price is a monthly rate, 1 to 3½ per cent, and the conditioning event is the passage of
time rather than the completion of a passage.

That is a different economic object — a revolving credit on the hull at sea-risk, not a
voyage bottomry — and on the surface it has a better claim to a row than the insurance-
referenced group rejected in logbook 2. **It was not created, on the diversity budget and
on the `.NR` rule together.** Six acts, three of them the same lender (Gerrit Bruynsen) and
two the same borrower (Jacob Beek of Alkmaar), is a counterparty relationship, and the
voyage acts are silent rather than contrary on free navigation. **Falsification condition:
a second corpus, from another port or another decade, in which time-priced bodemerij with
unrestricted navigation appears among unrelated counterparties.** At that point the
question is whether `DR1` is doing the work or whether the census needs the form.

### Two things checked and found not to be findings

**The armed merchantman is not evidence of a new hazard class.** No. 1995's six *gootelingen*
and four *steenstucken*, and the artillery quarrel in no. 1992, were briefly attractive as
evidence that the priced peril was adversarial rather than natural. They are already
inside `HZ2=P` and add nothing the characteristic does not hold.

**The multi-lender acts are not pooling.** No. 956 has four lenders on one hull, sharing
Dunkirker loss "penninck ponts gelijck" and expressly ranking *pari passu* — "even naer

### Result discarded: the intertemporal-mitigation reading of the avarız fund

Before the sources were read, a vault note argued that the *avarız* endowment was a
**buffer stock** mitigating a **perfectly correlated** peril — that a levy assessed on a
quarter under collective liability has ρ→1 across its members, so cross-sectional pooling
is unavailable in principle, and the fund must therefore work by accumulating in ordinary
years and discharging in levy years. That would have made it mitigation **across time
rather than across members**, an axis `MC1` does not have, and it was proposed as the most
theoretically interesting thing in the census.

**Two falsification conditions were stated in the note. Both failed the same afternoon,
and the framing failed beneath them.**

**Condition one — the buffer reading requires drawdown of principal. It is categorically
forbidden.** Küçük 2025, 150: *"Para vakıflarında sarf işleminin kesinlikle elde edilen kâr
üzerinden yapılması gerekmektedir. Ana paradan harcama yapılmaması esası benimsenmiştir."*
Expenditure must absolutely be from the profit obtained; the principle that none be made
from the principal was adopted — enforced by personal liability on the *mütevelli* and even
on the officials collecting the *avarız akçesi*. At Kırkçeşme the quarter's needs were met
*from the nema*, 942 *kuruş* against 6,280 of untouchable corpus. **There is no buffer. It
is an endowment whose income substitutes for taxation.** Coded at `LS1=capped`.

**Condition two — a regularised levy is not a hazard. It was regularised.** Küçük 2025,
147–148: from the late sixteenth century the state, in fiscal crisis, resolved to collect
certain taxes continuously and in cash, and the *avârız-ı divâniye* and *tekâlif-i örfiye*
were "turned into regularly collected annual taxes."

**The framing was wrong beneath both conditions, and this is the part worth keeping.** The
note identified the wrong variable. The peril the fund addresses is not the levy but
**which household proves unable to meet its share** — Küçük's own account of the funds'
origin is that "difficulties began to be experienced in the process of the populace meeting
these newly created taxes", and the stated design is that "persons without the ability to
pay" benefit. That varies household by household and is **idiosyncratic**. Cross-sectional
mitigation was available all along. The levy is the setting; the incapacity is the hazard.
Coded at `HZ1=mixed`, since the fund also relieves earthquake, flood and fire.

**What replaced it, and why the exchange was favourable.** The reading that survives is not
about time at all: it is that donor and beneficiary sets are close to **disjoint**, which
is the census's third contributor–beneficiary disjunction and meets the falsification
condition recorded on 2026-08-12 for `MC1`. **A speculative axis was lost and an evidenced
vocabulary defect was gained.** The vault note is rewritten around the corrected argument
rather than deleted, on the standing principle that superseded reasoning stays auditable.

**Method note, since this is now the second time in two days.** Both discarded results were
produced by reasoning from a search engine's summary before opening a source — the Rouen
choir school on 2026-08-12, this on 2026-08-13. In both cases the correction came from the
first page of the first PDF. **The falsification conditions worked exactly as intended and
should keep being written down; what should change is that speculative notes are not written
until at least one source has been read in full.**
