## 4. What could not be coded, and why

Six `.NR` and three `.NA`, and the `.NR` are the substance of this batch.

**`AP1` entity shielding weak — not recorded, and the absence was measured.** Across all 349 pages `priority` occurs **0** times and `partition` **0** times; `creditor` occurs **13** times and all thirteen contexts were read — public creditors of the State (62–63, 71–72, 80), the arrest and bankruptcy regime facing an individual debtor (131), a corporation's inability to make further calls absent an express charter power (129), and the cost book adventurers' unlimited liability to external creditors (192). None asks whether entity creditors ranked ahead of members' personal creditors in entity assets. Harris's four features are legal personality, transferable joint stock, managerial hierarchy and limitation of liability (22, 110); creditor ranking is not among them and he never puts it.

**`AP2` liquidation protection — not recorded, and the reason is the increment the vocabulary already flags.** `AP2` has two limbs and the vocabulary records that it "OVERLAPS CI2 and may be redundant with it — AP2 adds the creditor dimension". Harris answers the members' limb, and that datum is coded at `CI2`. He never answers the creditors' limb. Carrying the `CI2` datum across would make `AP2` report `CI2` and nothing else, which is exactly the redundancy the flag exists to detect.

**`AP3` owner shielding and `LR1` liability extent — not recorded, on a vocabulary gap that is the source's own central finding.** This is the headline and it deserves the full statement. Harris denies in terms that the object had the shape these columns assume: *"it should not be assumed that the seventeenth or eighteenth centuries functioned under the modern perception of limitation of liability as a binary legal feature that a corporation or an era has or lacks. Limited liability in this period should not even be viewed as a continuum, but rather as a matrix with several dimensions"* (129), those dimensions being which of three classes of debt, by what procedure, at what time, up to what portion, and jointly or severally (128–129). `LR1`'s own question — "Were shareholders held in debt together or separately?" — is one of the questions he lists as unanswered.

Four further facts each independently block a value, and together they show this is not a temporal dodge:

1. The attribute is **instrument-dependent**. Calls on members required "call-making powers . . . explicitly given to that corporation in its act or charter"; incorporation by Act carried limited liability only "provided that an explicit clause to that affect was included in the incorporating statute" (129–130).
2. It is **expressly optional after 1825**, which is the opposite of the textbook story. The second operative clause of Copley's repeal act empowered the King to grant charters *without* limited liability, in terms: members "shall be individually liable, in their persons and property, for the debts, contracts and engagements of such corporation" (6 Geo. IV c.92, quoted at 270–271; and see 265). Its promoters "envisioned an increase in chartered incorporation, yet in most cases without the privilege of limited liability" (271).
3. It is **still not an attribute of incorporation in 1844**. Gladstone "removed the hotly disputed issue of limited liability from his agenda . . . and left the status quo in this matter untouched" (282), and after the act every complying undertaking enjoyed "all the other privileges of incorporation, **except for limited liability**" (285). General limited liability arrives in 1855–1856, outside this row's period.
4. The value would in any case **change inside the claimed period** — incoherent to the 1780s, "relatively clear by 1825 that incorporation by charter carried with it limitation on the liability of the shareholders" (130) — and heterogeneity across time is the condition for splitting a row, never for `P`.

**Splitting the row does not rescue these two cells, and that is the point.** A `1720–1825` / `1825–1844` split would leave `AP3` `.NR` in both phases, because on Harris's account owner shielding was at no date in scope an attribute of the *form* — only of the individual charter or act.

**`LR2` outcome coupling and `LR6` coupling symmetry — not recorded, and Harris rules the question out of scope himself.** He establishes that the corporation had "concentration of management" and a hierarchical managerial structure (22, 33) but never asks whether directors were exposed to the venture's outcomes, and says so: the book "deals with the formal institutional structure of managerial structure. It deals only briefly with the structure of share holding" (23 n 20), and he sets the separation-of-ownership-and-control literature aside as "less relevant to the earlier setting" (22 n 19). A whole-text sweep on `director|manager|managing` (51 pages) turned up nothing on their economic exposure; the one adjacent passage, MPs being offered directorships "to entrap the unwary" (263), is about parliamentary corruption. Inferring coupling from the shareholders' residual claim at `LR3` would be filling one column from the column next door. `LR6` follows `LR2`: `.NR`, not `.NA`, because `.NA` would assert `LR2=veiled`.

**The three `.NA` are structural.** `AP4` — no founder; the 2026-08-30 rule names charter among the modes that return `.NA`. `LR5` — `LR4=0`. `CF2` — `CF1=0`, so there is no labour-supplying party.

## 6. Adjudications for the maintainer

**6.1 The period. The open question is answered, and the answer is against both options as offered.** Neither 1600 nor 1720 is a change point in the *form* on Harris's own account: 1553/1555 is where joint stock and the joint-stock corporation begin, 1600 is the exception that survived, and 1720 is a legislative event Harris argues had minimal effect. **The proposal is `1720–1844` anyway, and the reason is the cells rather than the narrative.** Four coded characteristics take a different value before roughly 1680, so any earlier start would put the row inside the condition that split `voc` on 2026-08-29 and stripped `joint_stock` of its cells on 2026-09-06 — a type spanning both values of a characteristic:

- `CI1` — several-accounts before the 1620s (Russia Company reorganised 1586 on "several separate accounts", moving to "individual accounts" by 1622–1623, printed 45); common thereafter (33).
- `CI2` — no permanent stock before the 1680s (45); permanent stock in the corporations formed after the Restoration (52).
- `FP2` — monopoly "an almost inherent characteristic of the early business corporations" (41); "no longer an essential part of every incorporation" after the 1680s (52).
- `MG3` — charters limiting membership "by demanding relatively high membership fees" (41); open public subscription after 1720 (114).

1844 is the terminal date on the source's own account. **This is a scope call and it is the maintainer's to confirm.**

**6.2 Period and evidence are not co-extensive, and the row header says so.** The claimed period is 1720–1844; the coded cells rest on evidence spanning roughly 1660–1837, densest for 1766–1825. `LP1`, `LP2`, `LP3` and `TS1` rest on statements of the general law of corporations Harris dates from the sixteenth century together with acts of 1733 and 1766, so they are stable across and beyond the claimed period. `FP4` rests partly on 1786–1837 statutes and flips to `1` at 1844 by definition — the same weakness `deed_of_settlement_company` records for its own `FP4`. **Not one cell rests on evidence from 1720–1740.** A reviewer should read this as a row about c.1740–1830 that claims 1720–1844.

**6.3 The phase split I am not proposing.** The obvious repair for `AP3`/`LR1` is a `1720–1825` / `1825–1844` split. It does not work, for the reason in section 4: the value is instrument-dependent at every date, and the 1825 clause makes the instrument-dependence statutory rather than incidental. A split would produce two rows both `.NR` on those cells and would multiply the census's rows for nothing. **Recommendation: do not split.** If the maintainer wants a codable owner-shielding cell for an English chartered entity, the acquisition in section 9 is the route, not a split.

**6.4 The regulated corporation is excluded, and the exclusion is mine.** The maintainer's scope named the joint-stock chartered corporation. Harris treats the *regulated* corporation as an equally chartered business corporation, and it separates from this row on the census's own characteristics: its members "traded in their own stock, taking risks and liabilities individually" (32) — several-accounts and no shared residual — against the joint-stock corporation's single account (33). I excluded it on that distinction. If the maintainer wants it, it is a separate row and a cheap one, since chapter 2 carries it.

**6.5 `MG2` cannot record what this source thinks most important, and I am flagging it rather than repairing it.** Managerial hierarchy is one of Harris's four defining features of the corporation (22, 110), and what he says distinguishes it from the partnership is a "legally enforced arrangement for appointment and delineation of responsibilities of officeholders, and for assemblies, reports, votes, and the like" (144). `MG2`'s three values — `collective` / `founder-fixed` / `single-principal` — record the *locus* of decision and cannot record a delegated hierarchy under collective ultimate control. Coding `collective` is the only fit and it loses the feature. **This is a candidate well-formedness observation in Sereno's sense and it is not a proposal.** Proposing the repair in the same batch as the coding that motivates it is retrospective selection; the rule forbids it and I am not doing it. Recorded here so that a later batch with a second witness on managerial structure can pick it up.

**6.6 `LR4=0` is the softest cell in the fragment and the alternative is stated.** Harris defines the mutual against the joint-stock company on exactly the pooling question — mutual owners "were also the consumers or clients" (36) — and the Sun was "based on proprietorship and joint stock, not on mutual principles. The insured persons were not necessarily members" (101). But he also holds that mutuals "could be organized under various schemes: as corporations or as unincorporated firms" (33–36), so a chartered body could itself be a mutual, and the value holds for the *proprietary* joint-stock corporation this row scopes rather than for every chartered corporation. Confidence is `low` for that reason. **A maintainer who reads 33–36 as a taxonomy of forms rather than as evidence about this form should recode `LR4` to `.NR`, which carries `LR5` from `.NA` to `.NR` with it.** That is the one recode that would change two cells.

On a charter-only reading of the row that asymmetry pushes toward `P`.
