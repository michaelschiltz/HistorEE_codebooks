## 1. Test 1 — Ḥanafī deposit law on mixing: the hypothesis is qualified

**Predicted before reading:** in *Kitāb al-wadīʿa* a depositary's inseparable mixing counts as consumption (*istihlāk*), shifts liability (*ḍamān*), and turns the owner's property into an equivalent owed; co-ownership pro rata as the rule for unauthorised mixing would count against; a school disagreement between Abū Ḥanīfa and the two companions would qualify. **Read:** vol. 11, 108–112 and 118 (the deposit chapter's *khalṭ* passages), 91–92 (*ghaṣb*, to which the deposit chapter refers its rule); vol. 22, every *khalṭ*/*ikhtilāṭ* cluster: 32, 39–40, 48, 67, 76, 91, 96–97, 102, 131–136, 139–140, 143–145, 151–152, 157–158, 180–184.

**Found.** (i) The deposit chapter grounds liability for inseparable mixing in lost access, not consumption: *li-annahu yataʿadhdharu bihi ʿalā al-mālik al-wuṣūl ilā ʿayn milkihi* (11, 110); easily separable mixing is *mujāwara laysa bi-khalṭ*, no liability; separable with hardship counts as inseparable. (ii) Consumption is Abū Ḥanīfa's: *bi'l-khalṭ ṣāra milk kull wāḥid minhumā mustahlakan ḥukman*, liability then vesting ownership in the mixer (*ghaṣb*, 11, 92); in the deposit chapter, *al-ẓāhir min madhhab Abī Ḥanīfa al-makhlūṭ milk li'l-khāliṭ wa-ḥaqquhumā fī dhimmatihi* (11, 111). Abū Yūsuf, Muḥammad, and al-Ḥasan's report from Abū Ḥanīfa: *milk al-mālik lā yanqaṭiʿu ʿan al-makhlūṭ bal lahu al-khiyār bayna al-sharika fī al-makhlūṭ wa-bayna taḍmīn al-khāliṭ* (11, 111; 11, 92). Al-Sarakhsī's *aṣaḥḥ*: even on Abū Ḥanīfa's view *al-ḥaqq fīhi bāqin* until the substitute is paid (11, 111). (iii) The *muḍāraba* chapter states the ground disjunctively: *al-khalṭ innamā yakūnu mūjiban li'l-ḍamān bi-iʿtibār anna fīhi maʿnā al-istihlāk li-māl rabb al-māl aw maʿnā al-sharika fīhi* (22, 132; restated 133); at 22, 39 the second limb alone, *ījāb al-sharika … ʿalā wajh lam yarḍa bihi rabb al-māl*. (iv) Insolvency (22, 143): on Abū Ḥanīfa's view *ṣārat al-amāna daynan ʿalayhi* and the investor ranks pro rata with the creditors; on the companions', *yaṣīru ḍāminan wa-lākin lā yaṣīru mutamallikan*, and the investor may elect co-ownership and take his half. (v) Profit follows ownership: *al-ribḥ namāʾ al-māl fa-yakūnu milkan li-ṣāḥib al-māl … fa-lā yastaḥiqquhu ghayruhu illā bi'l-sharṭ* (22, 144); after unauthorised mixing *bi'l-ḍamān yamliku dhālika al-māl fa-yamliku ribḥahu*, to be given in alms as *kasb khabīth* (Abū Ḥanīfa and Muḥammad; Abū Yūsuf contra) (22, 133, 158); the *muḍāraba* lapses because its condition fails, *kawn raʾs al-māl amāna fī yadihi* (22, 48, 133). ***Ribā* is not invoked in any passage read.** (vi) The wrong is the act: mixing that arises *ḥukman*, by a single purchase with both funds or by receiving proceeds mixed, is no contravention and leaves co-ownership pro rata (22, 48, 133–134); mixing two *muḍāraba*s of one investor is none either, *innamā khalaṭa māl rabb al-māl bi-mālihi* (22, 132, 157–158); dyeing with his own dye makes him liable as a usurper, but the *muḍāraba* survives in the goods (22, 76, 182–183). (vii) Accidental mixing in the depositary's chest: no liability, *al-mukhtaliṭ mushtarak baynahumā bi-qadr milkihimā*, loss shared and the rest divided pro rata (11, 110).

**Ruling: qualifies.** The liability half holds for all three jurists: unauthorised mixing is a *ḍamān* event that ends the trust, where the Latin texts make the same act lawful conduct, and the coded `CI5` contrast stands. Three corrections to the note: (a) title extinction by mixing (*istihlāk ḥukmī*) is Abū Ḥanīfa's view, the *ẓāhir* of the school; the companions keep the owner's title with an option of *shirka*; (b) the note's *ribā* step is replaced by the text's ground: profit is the growth of owned property, liability transfers ownership to the mixer, so the profit is his (impure), and the *muḍāraba* lapses because the capital is no longer an *amāna*; (c) the wrong is the act of mixing, not the state of commingled holdings. **Strongest objection answered:** Ḥanafī doctrine follows *ẓāhir al-riwāya*, and on Abū Ḥanīfa's view the mechanism is stated in terms, creditor consequence included (22, 143). But al-Sarakhsī's own ground for the *muḍāraba* rule is the disjunction of (iii), and at 22, 39 the second limb alone, so the rule does not depend on extinction of title. On the companions' view the investor in a mixed fund holds a pro-rata share, as in Latin law, and the traditions differ only in who may decide to mix: unconsented *shirka* is a wrong in one and a default in the other. The note's "exceptions" paragraph becomes the main mechanism. **Non-independence:** one jurist reporting one school's founders; one Ḥanafī datum. Bearing on test 2: 22, 32 and 140 deny the investor any profit on the agent's own money, *ṭamaʿ fī ghayr maṭmaʿ li-annahu laysa lahu fī māl al-ʿāmil raʾs māl wa-lā ʿamal*. No cell changes; the vault note is revised.

## 2. Test 2 — Mālikī and Shāfiʿī law on mixing in the *qirāḍ*: counts against

**Predicted before reading (as qualified by ruling 1):** all three schools bar the agent's unauthorised mixing with liability, on a ground of ownership or consent; against: a school letting the agent mix freely, or a *ribā* ground; qualifying: mixing allowed under conditions, or mixed grounds.

**Found.** (i) **Mālikī law lets the agent mix without liability.** *Mudawwana* 3, 641, *fī al-ʿāmil al-muqāriḍ yakhliṭu mālahu bi'l-qirāḍ*: the agent who also trades his own money should mix the two and buy with both, *al-ṣawāb min dhālika an yakhliṭahumā jamīʿan thumma yashtarī bihimā jamīʿan* (Ibn al-Qāsim); mixing the investor did not stipulate, *lā taḍmanu lahu* (Mālik); buying with both funds, *lā baʾs bi-dhālika*, the goods held pro rata between the *qirāḍ*'s capital and what the agent paid. At 645 the agent may mix two investors' funds *min ghayr sharṭ … wa-lā ḍamān ʿalayhi*. (ii) **The Mālikī bar is on the stipulation:** *lā yaṣluḥu an yuqāriḍahu ʿalā an yakhliṭa al-muqāriḍ mālahu bi-māl al-qirāḍ* (641), because the investor *qad jarra ilā nafsihi manfaʿat māl ghayr mālihi* (648; 647: *manfaʿa li-nafsihi min ghayr ribḥ al-māl*); Saḥnūn files it under *ishtirāṭ al-ziyāda* (650). *Ribā* is not named. (iii) Mixing a second *qirāḍ* of the same investor into a first that has gained or lost is barred as *khaṭar bayyin*, since one fund's loss would be made good from the other's profit; before trading it is permitted (650–651). (iv) **The *Muwaṭṭaʾ* has no mixing rule.** No. 2035 (*bāb al-taʿaddī*) treats buying beyond the capital with the agent's own money: the investor may take the goods and repay the excess, or the agent is *sharīk lahu bi-ḥiṣṣatihi … fī al-namāʾ wa'l-nuqṣān*; the *Mudawwana* repeats it (637). (v) **Al-Shāfiʿī states no mixing rule** in the passages read: the *Mukhtaṣar* assumes the agent may travel with his own money, expenses *ʿalā qadr al-mālayn bi'l-ḥiṣaṣ* (3297); *al-Umm* argues against a fixed term on a consent ground, *ishtaraytu bi-mālī wa-mālika ghayr mufarraq wa-laʿallī lā arḍā bi-sharikatika fīhi* (1143). (vi) **Pryor 1977, 30** says the jurists "did not discuss the bilateral *qirāḍ* at all", which the *Mudawwana* (641, 647–648) and al-Sarakhsī (22, 32, 140) contradict; his n. 85 renders Gaiani's *li-stighzār al-shirāʾ* as "the usurious character", where the *Mudawwana* (648) means the increase of purchasing and gives as ground the investor's benefit from property not his own.

**Ruling: counts against.** The bar on unauthorised mixing is Ḥanafī, not Islamic. Mālikī law lets the agent mix his own money with the *qirāḍ* without the investor's leave and without liability, and the commingled goods are held pro rata; al-Shāfiʿī is silent in what was read. What survives: the note's title holds of Ḥanafī law, and its premise that profit rests on ownership holds in Mālikī law too (648). What fails: a Latin–Islamic split explained by trust law, since the *muḍārib* is an *amīn* in every school and the mechanism predicted the bar in every school. **Strongest objection answered:** if Mālikī law treats a mixture as co-owned rather than consumed, ruling 1's refinement (the law of mixtures and who may decide to mix) would predict Mālikī permissiveness. That is a new hypothesis, not the one tested; it is checkable in the Mālikī deposit and usurpation chapters, taken up under test 3. **Non-independence:** the *Muwaṭṭaʾ* and the *Mudawwana* are one school, and the *Mudawwana* is Ibn al-Qāsim reporting Mālik: one Mālikī datum against one Ḥanafī datum, with Shāfiʿī silence.
