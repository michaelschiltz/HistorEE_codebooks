## 1. Test 1 — Ḥanafī deposit law on mixing: the hypothesis is qualified

**Predicted before reading:** in *Kitāb al-wadīʿa* a depositary's inseparable mixing counts as consumption (*istihlāk*), shifts liability (*ḍamān*), and turns the owner's property into an equivalent owed; co-ownership pro rata as the rule for unauthorised mixing would count against; a school disagreement between Abū Ḥanīfa and the two companions would qualify. **Read:** vol. 11, 108–112 and 118 (the deposit chapter's *khalṭ* passages), 91–92 (*ghaṣb*, to which the deposit chapter refers its rule); vol. 22, every *khalṭ*/*ikhtilāṭ* cluster: 32, 39–40, 48, 67, 76, 91, 96–97, 102, 131–136, 139–140, 143–145, 151–152, 157–158, 180–184.

**Found.** (i) The deposit chapter grounds liability for inseparable mixing in lost access, not consumption: *li-annahu yataʿadhdharu bihi ʿalā al-mālik al-wuṣūl ilā ʿayn milkihi* (11, 110); easily separable mixing is *mujāwara laysa bi-khalṭ*, no liability; separable with hardship counts as inseparable. (ii) Consumption is Abū Ḥanīfa's: *bi'l-khalṭ ṣāra milk kull wāḥid minhumā mustahlakan ḥukman*, liability then vesting ownership in the mixer (*ghaṣb*, 11, 92); in the deposit chapter, *al-ẓāhir min madhhab Abī Ḥanīfa al-makhlūṭ milk li'l-khāliṭ wa-ḥaqquhumā fī dhimmatihi* (11, 111). Abū Yūsuf, Muḥammad, and al-Ḥasan's report from Abū Ḥanīfa: *milk al-mālik lā yanqaṭiʿu ʿan al-makhlūṭ bal lahu al-khiyār bayna al-sharika fī al-makhlūṭ wa-bayna taḍmīn al-khāliṭ* (11, 111; 11, 92). Al-Sarakhsī's *aṣaḥḥ*: even on Abū Ḥanīfa's view *al-ḥaqq fīhi bāqin* until the substitute is paid (11, 111). (iii) The *muḍāraba* chapter states the ground disjunctively: *al-khalṭ innamā yakūnu mūjiban li'l-ḍamān bi-iʿtibār anna fīhi maʿnā al-istihlāk li-māl rabb al-māl aw maʿnā al-sharika fīhi* (22, 132; restated 133); at 22, 39 the second limb alone, *ījāb al-sharika … ʿalā wajh lam yarḍa bihi rabb al-māl*. (iv) Insolvency (22, 143): on Abū Ḥanīfa's view *ṣārat al-amāna daynan ʿalayhi* and the investor ranks pro rata with the creditors; on the companions', *yaṣīru ḍāminan wa-lākin lā yaṣīru mutamallikan*, and the investor may elect co-ownership and take his half. (v) Profit follows ownership: *al-ribḥ namāʾ al-māl fa-yakūnu milkan li-ṣāḥib al-māl … fa-lā yastaḥiqquhu ghayruhu illā bi'l-sharṭ* (22, 144); after unauthorised mixing *bi'l-ḍamān yamliku dhālika al-māl fa-yamliku ribḥahu*, to be given in alms as *kasb khabīth* (Abū Ḥanīfa and Muḥammad; Abū Yūsuf contra) (22, 133, 158); the *muḍāraba* lapses because its condition fails, *kawn raʾs al-māl amāna fī yadihi* (22, 48, 133). ***Ribā* is not invoked in any passage read.** (vi) The wrong is the act: mixing that arises *ḥukman*, by a single purchase with both funds or by receiving proceeds mixed, is no contravention and leaves co-ownership pro rata (22, 48, 133–134); mixing two *muḍāraba*s of one investor is none either, *innamā khalaṭa māl rabb al-māl bi-mālihi* (22, 132, 157–158); dyeing with his own dye makes him liable as a usurper, but the *muḍāraba* survives in the goods (22, 76, 182–183). (vii) Accidental mixing in the depositary's chest: no liability, *al-mukhtaliṭ mushtarak baynahumā bi-qadr milkihimā*, loss shared and the rest divided pro rata (11, 110).

**Ruling: qualifies.** The liability half holds for all three jurists: unauthorised mixing is a *ḍamān* event that ends the trust, where the Latin texts make the same act lawful conduct, and the coded `CI5` contrast stands. Three corrections to the note: (a) title extinction by mixing (*istihlāk ḥukmī*) is Abū Ḥanīfa's view, the *ẓāhir* of the school; the companions keep the owner's title with an option of *shirka*; (b) the note's *ribā* step is replaced by the text's ground: profit is the growth of owned property, liability transfers ownership to the mixer, so the profit is his (impure), and the *muḍāraba* lapses because the capital is no longer an *amāna*; (c) the wrong is the act of mixing, not the state of commingled holdings. **Strongest objection answered:** Ḥanafī doctrine follows *ẓāhir al-riwāya*, and on Abū Ḥanīfa's view the mechanism is stated in terms, creditor consequence included (22, 143). But al-Sarakhsī's own ground for the *muḍāraba* rule is the disjunction of (iii), and at 22, 39 the second limb alone, so the rule does not depend on extinction of title. On the companions' view the investor in a mixed fund holds a pro-rata share, as in Latin law, and the traditions differ only in who may decide to mix: unconsented *shirka* is a wrong in one and a default in the other. The note's "exceptions" paragraph becomes the main mechanism. **Non-independence:** one jurist reporting one school's founders; one Ḥanafī datum. Bearing on test 2: 22, 32 and 140 deny the investor any profit on the agent's own money, *ṭamaʿ fī ghayr maṭmaʿ li-annahu laysa lahu fī māl al-ʿāmil raʾs māl wa-lā ʿamal*. No cell changes; the vault note is revised.

## 2. Test 2 — Mālikī and Shāfiʿī law on mixing in the *qirāḍ*: counts against

**Predicted before reading (as qualified by ruling 1):** all three schools bar the agent's unauthorised mixing with liability, on a ground of ownership or consent; against: a school letting the agent mix freely, or a *ribā* ground; qualifying: mixing allowed under conditions, or mixed grounds.

**Found.** (i) **Mālikī law lets the agent mix without liability.** *Mudawwana* 3, 641, *fī al-ʿāmil al-muqāriḍ yakhliṭu mālahu bi'l-qirāḍ*: the agent who also trades his own money should mix the two and buy with both, *al-ṣawāb min dhālika an yakhliṭahumā jamīʿan thumma yashtarī bihimā jamīʿan* (Ibn al-Qāsim); mixing the investor did not stipulate, *lā taḍmanu lahu* (Mālik); buying with both funds, *lā baʾs bi-dhālika*, the goods held pro rata between the *qirāḍ*'s capital and what the agent paid. At 645 the agent may mix two investors' funds *min ghayr sharṭ … wa-lā ḍamān ʿalayhi*. (ii) **The Mālikī bar is on the stipulation:** *lā yaṣluḥu an yuqāriḍahu ʿalā an yakhliṭa al-muqāriḍ mālahu bi-māl al-qirāḍ* (641), because the investor *qad jarra ilā nafsihi manfaʿat māl ghayr mālihi* (648; 647: *manfaʿa li-nafsihi min ghayr ribḥ al-māl*); Saḥnūn files it under *ishtirāṭ al-ziyāda* (650). *Ribā* is not named. (iii) Mixing a second *qirāḍ* of the same investor into a first that has gained or lost is barred as *khaṭar bayyin*, since one fund's loss would be made good from the other's profit; before trading it is permitted (650–651). (iv) **The *Muwaṭṭaʾ* has no mixing rule.** No. 2035 (*bāb al-taʿaddī*) treats buying beyond the capital with the agent's own money: the investor may take the goods and repay the excess, or the agent is *sharīk lahu bi-ḥiṣṣatihi … fī al-namāʾ wa'l-nuqṣān*; the *Mudawwana* repeats it (637). (v) **Al-Shāfiʿī states no mixing rule** in the passages read: the *Mukhtaṣar* assumes the agent may travel with his own money, expenses *ʿalā qadr al-mālayn bi'l-ḥiṣaṣ* (3297); *al-Umm* argues against a fixed term on a consent ground, *ishtaraytu bi-mālī wa-mālika ghayr mufarraq wa-laʿallī lā arḍā bi-sharikatika fīhi* (1143). (vi) **Pryor 1977, 30** says the jurists "did not discuss the bilateral *qirāḍ* at all", which the *Mudawwana* (641, 647–648) and al-Sarakhsī (22, 32, 140) contradict; his n. 85 renders Gaiani's *li-stighzār al-shirāʾ* as "the usurious character", where the *Mudawwana* (648) means the increase of purchasing and gives as ground the investor's benefit from property not his own.

**Ruling: counts against.** The bar on unauthorised mixing is Ḥanafī, not Islamic. Mālikī law lets the agent mix his own money with the *qirāḍ* without the investor's leave and without liability, and the commingled goods are held pro rata; al-Shāfiʿī is silent in what was read. What survives: the note's title holds of Ḥanafī law, and its premise that profit rests on ownership holds in Mālikī law too (648). What fails: a Latin–Islamic split explained by trust law, since the *muḍārib* is an *amīn* in every school and the mechanism predicted the bar in every school. **Strongest objection answered:** if Mālikī law treats a mixture as co-owned rather than consumed, ruling 1's refinement (the law of mixtures and who may decide to mix) would predict Mālikī permissiveness. That is a new hypothesis, not the one tested; it is checkable in the Mālikī deposit and usurpation chapters, taken up under test 3. **Non-independence:** the *Muwaṭṭaʾ* and the *Mudawwana* are one school, and the *Mudawwana* is Ibn al-Qāsim reporting Mālik: one Mālikī datum against one Ḥanafī datum, with Shāfiʿī silence.

## 4. Test 3 — Islamic agency and deposit: supports the hypothesis as restated

**Predicted before reading:** (a) a Ḥanafī *wakīl* who mixes the principal's money with his own is *ḍāmin* on the deposit and *muḍāraba* ground, an agent free to mix counting against; (b) for the refinement left open by ruling 2, Mālikī deposit law treats like-with-like mixing as non-culpable or co-owned, Mālikī deposit liability for such mixing counting against the refinement; (c) Shāfiʿī deposit, no separate prediction.

**Found.** (i) *Kitāb al-wakāla* has no mixing rule (its one *khalṭ* hit, 19, 46, is an agent's derangement, *ikhtalaṭa ʿaqluhu*), but it holds the *wakīl* an *amīn* throughout (19, 10, 19, 49, 68, 71) and money he collects *kāna bi-manzilat al-wadīʿa ʿinda al-wakīl* (19, 74): the deposit rule (11, 110–111) governs him by assimilation, not by a rule read. (ii) The *Mudawwana*'s *wakālāt* chapter is silent on mixing. (iii) **Mālikī deposit** (*Mudawwana* 4, 434–435): dirhams mixed with the depositary's own and lost, *lā arā ʿalayhi ḍamānan* (Ibn al-Qāsim, *fī raʾyī*; likewise dinars), because *innamā hiya darāhim wa-darāhim, fa-li-hādhā minhā bi-qadr darāhimihi wa-li-hādhā minhā bi-qadr darāhimihi*, if equal in quality; like wheat mixed *ʿalā wajh al-rafʿ wa'l-ḥirz*, no liability *fī qawl Mālik*; wheat with barley, or with unlike wheat, liable, *atlafahā ḥīna khalaṭahā* (434); on partial loss the owners *yakūnāni sharīkayn fīmā baqiya* pro rata (435); a depositary who spends part and restores the like is discharged (Mālik, 435), where Ḥanafī law holds him for the whole (11, 111). (iv) **Shāfiʿī deposit** (*Umm*, *al-wadīʿa*; continuous p. 1330): gold mixed with his silver, liable for diminution only; with gold still distinguishable, not liable on loss; *lā yatamayyazu minhā tamayyuzan bayyinan*, liable; an indistinguishable replacement coin makes him liable for the whole.

**Ruling: supports, the hypothesis restated.** Within each school the trustee's mixing rule is one rule across deposit, agency (Ḥanafī, by assimilation) and *qirāḍ*: Ḥanafī liable in all three; Mālikī not liable for like-with-like mixing, co-ownership pro rata, in deposit and *qirāḍ*; Shāfiʿī liable in deposit, *qirāḍ* unread. **Restated hypothesis:** the commenda family's mixing default follows each tradition's law of mixtures for fungibles held in trust; where like-with-like mixing leaves the owner a pro-rata share the agent may mix (Mālikī; Latin pending test 5), where it makes the mixer liable he may not (Ḥanafī; Shāfiʿī predicted, unread). **Strongest objection answered:** the Mālikī grounds are contract-specific (safekeeping in deposit, avoiding a price fall in the *qirāḍ*, 641), so the two rules could coincide by chance; the shared element is the property consequence, a pro-rata share in both (435, 641), against liability in Ḥanafī and Shāfiʿī deposit law, which is why this is supports, not confirms. **MS's reservation, recorded at his ruling: he suspects the two Mālikī contract-specific rules coincide by chance.** Limits: the Ḥanafī agency rule is reached by assimilation; the Mālikī dirham ruling is Ibn al-Qāsim's opinion; the predicted Shāfiʿī *qirāḍ* rule is unread (a later Shāfiʿī text, such as al-Shīrāzī's *Muhadhdhab*, would be an out-of-sample check; open, not taken up). **Non-independence:** one text per school, three data points. No cell.

## 5. Test 4 — the Venetian *colleganza*: qualifies (statutory silence)

**Predicted before reading:** commingling of the *colleganza* capital with the traveller's own, gain and loss *per libram*, and no liability for mixing as such; against: a Venetian duty to keep the capital apart or liability for unauthorised mixing; qualifying: silence.

**Found.** The text layer of the whole tome has no hit for the mixing verbs (*misc-*, *commisc-*, *confund-*, *mescol-*), for *libra* in a *colleganza* context, or for *separat-*, *seorsum*, *de suo*. (i) Ranieri Dandolo's *Nove constitutiones* (c. 1202–1205), c. 16, *Capitulum de colegantiis obseruandis* (251): *ille qui ab aliquo pecuniam, ut cum ipsa lucretur, acceperit, nichil in colegantia mittat, sed usque ad tempus statutum cum pecunia accepta procertans, quartam partem uel quantum in cartulis continebitur sibi teneat de prouentu. Reliquas uero partes simul cum capitanea more solito dare debeat creditori* — the unilateral *colleganza*'s division of profit; *nichil in colegantia mittat* reads either as the taker contributing nothing or as his not passing the money on in *colleganza*, and neither reading reaches his own trading stock. (ii) Tiepolo's *novae leges* of 3 July 1229, c. 16 (273): the taker accounts *seriatim … qualiter inuestiuerit et uendiderit, et qualiter egerit de hoc quod habuit in colegantia* and confirms it by oath, shipwreck and robbery excepted — a duty to account, not to segregate. (iii) St. Enr. Dand. 30–32 (221–223; read 2026-10-12): delay, the oath for loss, the investor's proof that the traveller *de habere tamen collegantie habuerit*. (iv) Besta's preface (112) reports *St. Nov.* III, 3 (1242): all *colleganza* charters of one debtor to one or several investors for the same voyage have equal force in collection regardless of date; the 1242 text is outside the edition and was not read.

**Ruling: qualifies.** The statutes neither confirm nor contradict commingling *per libram*; nothing points to segregation. Venice adds nothing to the Latin datum on mixing; the question goes to the acts. Morozzo della Rocca & Lombardo 1940 (`GXRRAIUK`) is not held: no copy on archive.org; HathiTrust's catalogue refuses automated access and was not checked; no other open copy found; nothing downloaded. **Strongest objection answered:** read as "he puts nothing in, but works with the money received", the clause might confine the taker to the money received; but its object is the *colleganza*, its subject the division of profit, and a confinement rule would have to name his own goods, as Cassinese's *separatim* and *Consolat* cap. 215 do. **Non-independence:** Venice shares the Roman-law substrate; any confirmation from the acts would join the one Latin datum. No cell.

## 6. Test 5 — Roman law on mixture: qualifies; the Latin default tracks the consensual rule, and for coins Roman law sides with Abū Ḥanīfa

**Predicted before reading:** Roman law leaves each owner a share in a mixture of fungibles, consensual mixing giving co-ownership pro rata; against: a Roman rule giving the mixture to the mixer or ending the other owner's title on unconsented mixing; qualifying: a split by consent or mode, the Latin default then tracking the consensual case only.

**Found.** (i) Consent makes the mass common: *Si duorum materiae ex voluntate dominorum confusae sint, totum id corpus … utriusque commune est* (Inst. 2.1.27); *Voluntas duorum dominorum miscentium materias commune totum corpus efficit* (D. 41.1.7.8); grain mixed *ex voluntate vestra* is common, the grains *ex consensu vestro communicata* (Inst. 2.1.28; D. 6.1.5 pr., *communicata videbuntur et erit communi dividundo actio*). (ii) Without consent the result turns on mode and matter: *confusio* by chance, the same (D. 41.1.7.9; Inst. 2.1.27); grain mixed by chance *vel Titius id miscuerit sine voluntate tua*, *non videtur commune esse, quia singula corpora in sua substantia durant*, each owner suing *pro modo frumenti cuiusque* (Inst. 2.1.28; D. 6.1.5 pr.); two owners' silver melted into a mass, *erit nobis commune, et unusquisque pro rata ponderis … vindicabimus* (Pomponius, D. 6.1.3.2), and whoever caused it *dolo malo* is liable for theft (Paul, D. 6.1.4). (iii) **Coins:** *Si alieni nummi inscio vel invito domino soluti sunt, manent eius cuius fuerunt: si mixti essent, ita ut discerni non possent, eius fieri qui accepit … ita ut actio domino cum eo, qui dedisset, furti competeret* (Iavolenus after Gaius, D. 46.3.78): title ends and the owner keeps a personal claim.

**Ruling: qualifies.** The Latin *per libram* default tracks Roman law's consensual rule, the commenda supplying the investor's consent by contract or statute (Genoese default, *Consolat* cap. 215, Pisa's *statim communicet*). The note's claim that Roman mixing preserves ownership as a share is false for unconsented mixing of coins, where Roman law and Abū Ḥanīfa agree. **Consequence for ruling 4:** on the Latin–Ḥanafī axis the difference is not in the law of mixtures but in who is presumed to consent: Latin commenda law supplies the investor's consent by default, Ḥanafī law requires it expressly (*iʿmal fīhi bi-raʾyika*). The mechanism returns to ruling 1's "who may decide to mix". The property-law difference that survives is Mālikī deposit law (like-with-like mixing without consent leaves co-ownership), which Roman law gives for weighed silver but not for counted coin; this is consistent with MS's reservation at ruling 4. **Strongest objection answered:** D. 46.3.78 concerns payment with another's coins, not a bailee's mixing, and D. 6.1.3.2 gives co-ownership for inseparable mixing whatever the consent; but the commenda capital is counted coin, D. 46.3.78 is the only text read on coins mixed beyond telling apart, and D. 6.1.3.2 concerns metal melted and claimed by weight; D. 6.1.4's theft liability for the fraudulent mixer is the Roman analogue of *ḍamān*. **Open, not read:** the *depositum irregulare* texts (D. 16.3.25.1, D. 12.1.9.9, D. 12.1.10). **Non-independence:** one Roman corpus, under the one Latin datum. No cell.
