1. **Training.** What I recall, stated as recall, not as evidence, and with no claim that any of it is right:
   - *Terms.* The Venetian *colleganza* (*collegantia*, *colegancia*) in two shapes: (a) the investor (*stans*) supplies all the capital, the travelling party (*tractator*, *procertans*) the labour, profit divided three-quarters to the investor and one-quarter to the traveller; (b) the investor supplies two-thirds and the traveller one-third, profit divided by halves. Capital lost to the peril "of sea and of people" (*maris et gentis*), if it is shown openly and without fraud, discharges the traveller; a traveller who fails to render account and return capital and profit within a set time (I recall thirty days after return) owes double (*duplum*) and interest of 20% a year ("de quinque sex per annum"). Other drafting terms I recall: *rogadia* (goods carried for another without a share of profit), *commendacio* (deposit or entrustment), *prestitum* and *mutuum* (loans, often at "quinque sex"), sea loans repayable at or after arrival and "salvum in terra" (at the lender's risk at sea), *securitas* (release), *procura* / *committens committo* (mandate). I recall that in Venice the single word *collegantia* covered both shapes, where Genoese notaries distinguished *societas* (bilateral) from *commenda/accomendacio* (unilateral); I hold that with medium confidence.

2. Not uniform. 3. ## 3. How you will assign an act to a form

1. *`societas_maris`:* an act in which the traveller states that he puts in capital of his own beside the investor's (amounts stated for both; typically two-thirds/one-third) and profit is divided by halves (*per medium*) or otherwise in proportion to capital plus a labour share. *`commenda_alloc`:* an act in which one party supplies all the capital and the other carries and trades it, with profit divided (typically three-quarters/one-quarter) and the investor bearing the peril (*maris et gentis*). For the loss cells I read the peril clause, any loss or discharge clause, the profit rule and the return/penalty clause.
2. No. I expect *collegantia* to name both shapes. An act called *collegantia* with no capital from the traveller I assign to `commenda_alloc`, not `societas_maris`. An act fitting both (e.g. a traveller who puts in a small sum, or several investors of whom one travels) I record as `both` with the deciding clause, and I report the cells with and without it. An act fitting neither (*rogadia*, fixed-return loans, deposits) is `neither`. The name of the act is recorded in `act_type_latin` and never decides the form.
3. Yes, conditionally. A release is evidence for a cell only where it recites the term the cell asks about: the contributions (for `CF1`, `CI1`, `CF3`), the voyage (`TS2`), a loss and its discharge (`RB1`, `RB4`), or the profit division. A release reciting only a sum and *de collegancia* goes to the census and the stratum's counts and to no cell; I will also not count it towards a cell's n.
